# Archived weather forecasts for Peru at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks

Investigation of whether archived weather and sub-seasonal climate forecasts with original issue times exist for Peru at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks (28 days ahead on MINSA's Sunday-to-Saturday epi-week calendar), whether they can enter the national district (6-digit UBIGEO) by epi-week panel without leakage, and whether sub-seasonal forecast skill over Peru (validated first on the pilot: Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque) justifies carrying forecast columns alongside the observed climate and ENSO sources settled in issue #18 (ERA5-Land, CHIRPS v3.0, and NOAA CPC weekly OISST v2.1). All cloud buckets, API catalogues, licences, directory listings, and literature abstracts cited below were verified directly on 2026-11-23 UTC.

## Short answer

1. **No public archive provides a clean, consistent operational weather forecast record from 2000 to 2026 at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks.** Real-time operational archives that reach 28+ days start late: NOAA CFSv2 in April 2011 (on NCEI; October 2018 on AWS), ECMWF S2S in January 2015 (on ECDS at `1.5°`), NOAA GEFSv12 on 23 September 2020 (on AWS and dynamical.org), and dynamical.org's ECMWF 46-day mirror on 1 January 2026. Medium-range open archives (ECMWF Open Data, CHC CHIRPS3-GEFS, Open-Meteo Single Runs, ECMWF AIFS, Google WeatherNext 2) stop at 15 to 16 days and cannot reach horizons 3 and 4 epi-weeks.
2. **Splicing reforecasts onto operational forecasts creates train/serve skew and gaps.** NOAA GEFSv12 reforecasts (`2000-01-01` to `2019-12-31`, 11 members on Wednesdays only) leave a nearly 9-month gap before operational GEFSv12 (`2020-09-23` onward, 31 members daily), differ in initial-condition assimilation (GEFSv12 reanalysis vs operational GDAS), and face a planned GEFSv13 cutover in Q2 FY2027. NOAA CFSv2 (`1999-2010` reforecasts plus `2011-present` operational) is the only daily sub-seasonal system with a continuous 2000 to 2026 frozen-model pair, but it is coarse (~56 to 100 km), has large biases over Peru, and its NCEI archive is disrupted by a 2026 cloud migration. Copernicus C3S ECMWF SEAS5.1 (`1981-2026`) is consistent on paper, but updates only monthly (released on the 6th at 12:00 UTC) at `1.0°` resolution and shifts from 25 hindcast members to 51 operational members in 2017.
3. **Sub-seasonal forecast skill over Peru at forecast weeks 2 to 4 is weak for rainfall and largely redundant with lagged observed climate for dengue at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks.** Peer-reviewed S2S evaluations over South America find substantial wet biases near the Andes from week 1, tercile rainfall skill ending after week 1, and unconditional correlation failing past week 1 over the Andes and Amazon. Global models also miss narrow Peruvian coastal upwelling and Niño 1+2 mesoscale structure. Meanwhile, vector incubation and transmission lags mean that observed temperature, rainfall, and weekly coastal SST through the issue week already capture most of the climate forcing relevant to horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks.
4. **Verdict: do not add archived weather forecasts to the v1 panel.** Keep the panel on lagged observed climate and weekly OISST ENSO indices from issue #18. Revisit forecast inputs only as a gated later experiment (starting with operational-only `2020-2026` NOAA GEFSv12 35-day Zarr or `2015-2026` ECDS ECMWF S2S) if pilot model ablations show horizons 3 and 4 suffer from missing forward climate signal, or if scope ever expands beyond horizon 4 epi-weeks.

## Source details and verification notes

### ECMWF extended range, ECMWF Open Data, and the S2S database

Three separate ECMWF distribution pathways are easily conflated and have very different coverage and access terms:

1. **ECMWF Open Data (`data.ecmwf.int/forecasts/` and `s3://ecmwf-forecasts/`)**: On 1 October 2025, ECMWF removed information charges on its Real-time Catalogue under CC BY 4.0, and on 20 October 2026 it added free 9 km (`0.1°`) medium-range fields ([ECMWF Open Data](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/open-data); [9 km announcement](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2026/9-km-free-and-open-data-offers-step-change-localised-forecasts)). Direct inspection of both `https://data.ecmwf.int/forecasts/20261122/00z/ifs/` and `s3://ecmwf-forecasts/20261122/00z/ifs/` confirms that only medium-range streams (`enfo`, `oper`, `waef`, `wave`, max forecast hour `360h` = 15 days) are published in the free direct-download buckets. Extended-range 46-day forecasts (`eefo`, Set VI) are absent from both `0p1` and `0p25` open-data prefixes. `data.ecmwf.int/forecasts/` retains only 4 rolling days (`20261120` to `20261123`), while AWS `s3://ecmwf-forecasts/` goes back to `2023-01-18`.
2. **ECMWF Set VI (`eefo`/`eefh`) and MARS archive**: Operational 46-day extended-range forecasts run daily at ~36 km with 101 ensemble members since IFS Cycle 48r1 in June 2023, paired with 20-year reforecasts (`eefh`) on odd calendar days ([Set VI](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/set-vi)). However, direct delivery of Set VI and access to the historical MARS archive require a separate ECMWF Service Agreement with associated delivery or archive service charges ([Service Agreements](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/accessing-forecasts/service-agreements)). `dynamical.org` mirrors the 101-member 46-day IFS ENS at `1.5°` under CC BY 4.0 without an account, but its archive starts only on `2026-01-01` ([STAC](https://stac.dynamical.org/ecmwf-ifs-ens-forecast-46-day-daily-1-5-degree/collection.json)).
3. **WWRP/WCRP S2S Prediction Project database (`s2s-forecasts` and `s2s-reforecasts`)**: Legacy `apps.ecmwf.int/datasets/data/s2s/` migrated to the ECMWF Data Store (ECDS) on 27 May 2026, and archiving continues through a 2024 to 2028 phase ([ECDS `s2s-forecasts`](https://ecds.ecmwf.int/datasets/s2s-forecasts)). Crucially, the legacy 3-week real-time embargo reported in older literature ([Vitart et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-16-0017.1)) is gone: ECMWF cut its own S2S delay to **48 hours** on 29 February 2024 ([ECMWF 2024 announcement](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2024/ecmwf-releases-much-larger-open-dataset)), and the official S2S licence PDF fetched from ECDS confirms a **48-hour delay** for 10 centres (including ECMWF and NCEP) and a **1-week delay** for BoM, CNRM, and UKMO ([S2S licence PDF](https://object-store.os-api.cci2.ecmwf.int:443/cci2-prod-catalogue/licences/s2s-licence/s2s-licence_c6d3b10fff18f2016c2bd4071748c73609d92c0d3cb4938132012457bdf77900.pdf)). Live inspection on `2026-11-23` confirmed `s2s-forecasts` runs through `2026-11-21` (literally 48 hours behind real time) and starts `2015-01-01`. Six centres (ECMWF, NCEP, ECCC, KMA, HMCR, UKMO) are **CC BY 4.0**; the other seven are **CC BY-NC 4.0**. Despite the improved embargo, the `1.5°` (~165 km) grid spans entire Peruvian departments in 1 to 2 pixels and operational history is missing for `2000-2014`.

### NOAA GEFSv12 operational and reforecast

Direct inspection of both AWS NODD buckets clarifies three details that secondary summaries frequently misstate:

1. **Operational 35-day cutover and release lag**: `s3://noaa-gefs-pds/` starts on `2017-01-01`, but runs through `2020-09-22` use the GEFSv11 layout (`pgrb2a/`) and stop at 16 days. Operational GEFSv12 (`atmos/pgrb2ap5/`, 31 members, 35 days = `f840` at `00 UTC`) begins on **`2020-09-23`** and runs continuously through `2026-11-23` ([AWS Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-gefs/)). Inspection of `geavg.t00z.pgrb2a.0p50.f840.idx` confirms `TMP:2 m`, `RH:2 m`, `TMAX`, `TMIN`, and `APCP` are present at day 35. Object `LastModified` times across three consecutive runs (`2026-11-20` to `2026-11-22`) show a two-stage operational release: `f384` (day 16) lands at `~06:30 UTC` (+6.5 hours), whereas `f390` starts around `00:55 UTC` the next day and **`f840` (day 35) finishes at `03:21` to `03:58 UTC` the next day (+27.5 to +28 hours)**. `dynamical.org` repackages this exact 31-member 35-day operational archive from `2020-10-01` to present in cloud-native Zarr v3 under CC BY 4.0 ([STAC](https://stac.dynamical.org/noaa-gefs-forecast-35-day/collection.json)).
2. **Reforecast archive scope and 2020 gap**: While [Guan et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-21-0245.1) describe a 31-year (`1989-2019`) GEFSv12 reforecast split into Phase 1 (`1989-1999`, CFSR initial conditions) and Phase 2 (`2000-2019`, GEFSv12 reanalysis initial conditions), direct listing of `s3://noaa-gefs-retrospective/GEFSv12/reforecast/` confirms only **`2000-01-01` to `2019-12-31`** is hosted on AWS (plus a stray `2020/2010070100/` typo prefix), consistent with NOAA's own storage PDF ([Description_of_reforecast_data.pdf](https://noaa-gefs-retrospective.s3.amazonaws.com/Description_of_reforecast_data.pdf)). Only Wednesday `00 UTC` runs extend to 35 days (11 members: `c00`, `p01` to `p10`); non-Wednesday runs stop at 16 days with 5 members. Surface 2 m humidity is stored as specific humidity (`spfh_2m`), not relative humidity. Between the last reforecast (`2019-12-31`) and the first operational GEFSv12 35-day run (`2020-09-23`), **nearly 9 months of 2020 have no GEFSv12 35-day forecasts at all**.
3. **Upcoming GEFSv13 discontinuity**: NOAA's Unified Forecast System roadmap plans a transition from GEFSv12 to coupled **GEFSv13** (targeted Q2 FY2027 in NOAA UFS schedules; exact operational cutover date UNCONFIRMED, with GEFSv12 verified still active in `s3://noaa-gefs-pds/` on `2026-11-23`; [EMC GEFS](https://www.emc.ncep.noaa.gov/emc/pages/numerical_forecast_systems/gefs.php)), which will end operational continuity with GEFSv12.

### NOAA CFSv2 and SubX / SubC

- **CFSv2** became operational on 30 March / 1 April 2011 and has remained a frozen coupled atmosphere-ocean model since ([Saha et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-12-00823.1); [NCEI CFS](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/weather-climate-models/climate-forecast-system)). Each day at `00, 06, 12, 18 UTC`, NCEP runs 1 member to 9 months and 3 members to 45 days (16 runs/day). Reforecasts cover `1982-2010` (9-month every 5 days) and `1999-2010` (45-day daily). Operational runs live at NCEI from `2011-04-01` to present and on AWS `s3://noaa-cfs-pds/` from `2018-10-31` to `2026-11-23` (verified live, including `prate`, `q2m`, `tmp2m`, and `ocnsst`, with `00 UTC` `time_grib_01` landing around `11:16 UTC`; note `cdas.` folders also continue in `noaa-cfs-pds` through November 2026). However, NCEI's product page displays an active 2026 cloud-migration warning noting that many access portals and data folders are unavailable or delayed ([NCEI CFS](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/weather-climate-models/climate-forecast-system)).
- **SubX / SubC**: The NOAA-funded Subseasonal Experiment ([Pegion et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-18-0270.1)) reorganized in 2023 into the Subseasonal Consortium ([SubC OU](https://weather.ou.edu/~kpegion/subc/)). Its `1.0°` multi-model database (`1999-2015` hindcasts plus `2017+` weekly real-time forecasts) is hosted at the IRI Data Library, which now enforces mandatory user authentication (`https://iridl.ldeo.columbia.edu/auth/login` verified live on `2026-11-23`). Because we did not create an account, exact 2026 per-model live availability and real-time latency behind the login gate remain UNCONFIRMED.

### CHC CHIRPS3-GEFS (and CHIRTS-GEFS)

As documented in [CHIRPS-GEFS/v3/README.txt](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS-GEFS/v3/README.txt) and the [CHC product page](https://www.chc.ucsb.edu/data/chirps-gefs), CHC discontinued `CHIRPS2-GEFS` on 1 July 2026 and now exclusively produces `CHIRPS3-GEFS` (`0.05°`, quantile-matched to CHIRPS v3 and temporally disaggregated with IMERG v7 Late). Because GEFSv12 has a January to September 2020 gap and IMERG starts in June 2000, CHC explicitly excludes both `2000` and `2020`, archiving **`2001-2019` and `2021-present`** (verified directly in `CHIRPS-GEFS/v3/16_day/`). Files are organized by original issue date (`daily/global/YYYY/MM/DD/`, `05_day/`, `10_day/`, `15_day/`, `16_day/`, `pentad_lead_0..2/`), and a sibling `CHIRTS-GEFS/v1/` directory holds 16-day temperature forecasts (its documentation URL returned 404). Two hard blockers rule it out for horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks: it **stops at 16 days** (covering horizons 1 to 2 epi-weeks only) and misses the entire year 2020.

### Open-Meteo APIs

Direct inspection of Open-Meteo's documentation and live API probes for Piura (`-5.1945, -80.6328`) rule out all four Open-Meteo forecast endpoints for a 2000 to 2026 backtest panel:

- **Historical Forecast API** ([docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-forecast-api)): starts around `2022` and stitches only the **first few hours** of each model run into a pseudo-analysis time series. It does not preserve 1-to-4-week forecast leads.
- **Previous Model Runs API** ([docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/previous-runs-api)): exposes fixed offsets `_previous_day1` through **`_previous_day7` only** (1 to 7 days ahead, missing horizons 2 to 4 epi-weeks), starting `January 2024` for most models (`March 2021` for GFS 2 m temperature).
- **Single Runs API** ([docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/single-runs-api)): retrieves full forecast horizons (up to 15 to 16 days) by `&run=` initialization time, but starts only in **March 2024** for ECMWF IFS 9 km and **2 April 2026** for all other models.
- **Seasonal Forecast API** ([docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/seasonal-forecast-api)): serves uncalibrated ECMWF SEAS5 / EC46 (36 km, 51 members), but has **no historical run archive**. A live probe on `2026-11-23` requesting `start_date=2025-01-01` returned HTTP `400`, and `past_days=365` returned `null` for every date before `2026-11-01` (the current month's run).
- **License constraint**: Although the underlying weather data are CC BY 4.0, Open-Meteo's free API tier is explicitly **non-commercial only** ([Terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms)).

### SENAMHI Peru and ENSO-specific outlooks

- **SENAMHI Peru**: Live inspection of `pronostico-meteorologico` ([SENAMHI](https://www.senamhi.gob.pe/?p=pronostico-meteorologico)) and `pronostico-climatico` ([SENAMHI](https://www.senamhi.gob.pe/?p=pronostico-climatico)) shows daily 3-to-5-day city forecasts and monthly-issued 3-month seasonal consensus tercile maps (e.g., November 2026 to January 2027 issued `2026-10-26`). Neither SENAMHI's web portal nor Peru's national open-data portal (`datosabiertos.gob.pe`) exposes a machine-readable historical archive of 1-to-4-epi-week numerical forecasts with original issue dates. Regional collaborations with CIIFEN and IRI's NextGen/PyCPT framework focus on calibrated seasonal and experimental sub-seasonal bulletins rather than an open multi-decade forecast archive.
- **Copernicus C3S Seasonal Forecasts (SEAS5.1)**, **NOAA CPC NMME**, and **IRI/CCSR ENSO Plume**: All three archive real-time monthly climate and ENSO outlooks with original issue months (SEAS5.1 `system=51` continuously `1981-2026` on CDS; NMME `realtime_anom/ENSMEAN/` verified live for 185 consecutive months `2011-08-08` to `2026-11-08`; IRI/CCSR monthly plumes `2002-2026` now hosted at [forecast.ccsr.columbia.edu](https://forecast.ccsr.columbia.edu/forecasts/enso/forecast)). However, all three update only **once per month** (6th, 8th, or ~19th of the month) and report coarse (`1.0°` or index-level) monthly/seasonal fields rather than weekly district-scale weather.

## Operational archives versus reforecasts and train/serve skew

Separating **operational archives** (produced in real time before the issue week) from **reforecasts** (generated retrospectively with a fixed model) exposes a structural dilemma across the 2000 to 2026 panel window:

1. **Operational-only archives are either too short or non-stationary.** Operational archives contain true point-in-time snapshots without hindsight bias, but only CFSv2 (2011+), ECDS S2S (2015+), and GEFSv12 (Sep 2020+) reach 28+ days. Except for frozen CFSv2, operational archives undergo periodic NWP upgrades (e.g., ECMWF IFS Cycles 41r1 through 49r1, including the June 2023 switch from twice-weekly 51-member runs to daily 101-member runs; GEFSv11 to GEFSv12 in September 2020 and upcoming GEFSv13 in Q2 FY2027). A dengue model trained across an upgrade boundary learns biases from an obsolete NWP version.
2. **Training on reforecasts and serving operational forecasts induces four forms of train/serve skew:**
   - **Ensemble-size and variance skew**: GEFSv12 reforecasts have 11 members vs 31 operational members; SEAS5.1 hindcasts have 25 members vs 51 operational members. Smaller ensembles produce noisier ensemble means and systematically different tail probabilities and spread-error relationships.
   - **Initial-condition assimilation skew**: Reforecasts initialize from homogeneous historical reanalyses (GEFSv12 reanalysis, CFSR, ERA5), whereas live operational runs initialize from real-time operational data assimilation (GDAS, ECMWF operational analysis, CDAS) whose observing network and error statistics evolve over time.
   - **Cadence and epi-week alignment skew**: GEFSv12 35-day reforecasts exist only on **Wednesdays at `00 UTC`**, whereas operational GEFSv12 runs daily. Matching MINSA's Sunday-to-Saturday epi-weeks forces live runs either to discard 3 to 4 days of fresher initializations (subsampling strictly to Wednesdays to match training) or to serve shorter effective forecast leads than training saw.
   - **Missing transition periods and model-replacement cliffs**: Splicing GEFSv12 reforecasts (`2000-2019`) onto operational GEFSv12 (`2020-09-23+`) leaves a nearly 9-month hole across 2020, and GEFSv13 (Q2 FY2027) will break parity again right as a production pipeline matures.
3. **Consistency check for 2000 to present**: **No source provides a consistent, high-resolution, weekly-updated operational+reforecast pair across 2000 to 2026.** Only two sources span 2000 to 2026 under one model ID:
   - **NOAA CFSv2** (`1999-2010` 45-day reforecasts + `2011-2026` operational): frozen model physics, but switches from CFSR to CDAS initializations in April 2011, has coarse resolution (~56 to 100 km) and large Andean/coastal biases, and faces 2026 NCEI archive disruption.
   - **C3S ECMWF SEAS5.1 (`system=51`)** (`1981-2016` hindcasts + `2017-2026` operational): consistent model version, but only monthly cadence (no weekly update between epi-weeks), `1.0°` resolution, and 25-to-51 member jump in 2017.

## Skill evidence for Peru and tropical South America at weeks 1 to 4

### Meteorological sub-seasonal skill (coast, Andes, and Amazon)

Peer-reviewed verifications consistently show that raw global sub-seasonal forecasts struggle over Peru at forecast weeks 2 to 4, especially for precipitation and over Andean topography:

- **Rapid rainfall skill decay over the Andes and Amazon**: Evaluating four S2S systems across South America during austral summer (November to March, 1999 to 2010), [Klingaman et al. (2020/2021)](https://doi.org/10.1175/WAF-D-19-0203.1) (*Weather and Forecasting* 36:147-169) found large mean biases in every model, including **wet biases near the Andes and dry biases in Amazonia**, established by week 1 and persisting through week 5. Unconditional temporal correlation **failed to extend past week 1 in the Andes and Amazonia** (reaching week 2 elsewhere and week 3 only over northern, northeastern, and southeastern South America). Probabilistic **Brier Skill Score against climatology for upper- and lower-tercile rainfall extended only to week 1**. Crucially, conditional skill during active MJO or ENSO phases was **not systematically higher** than unconditional skill because model teleconnection errors degraded regional rainfall forecasts even at short leads.
- **Need for calibration and regional contrast**: In global and South American S2S verifications, [de Andrade, Coelho and Cavalcanti (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00382-018-4457-z) (*Climate Dynamics* 52:5451-5475) and [Coelho, Firpo and de Andrade (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1127/metz/2018/0898) (*Meteorologische Zeitschrift* 27:503-520) showed that ECMWF leads other S2S centres, yet continental South American skill drops markedly from weeks 1 to 2 into weeks 3 to 4; the most skillful South American subregion is Northeast Brazil rather than western tropical South America, and raw ensembles require post-processing calibration for reliability.
- **Western tropical South America (Peru and Ecuador) and coastal Niño 1+2**: [Recalde-Coronel, Zaitchik and Pan (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00382-019-05107-2) (*Climate Dynamics* 54:2167-2185) documented MJO modulation of sub-seasonal rainfall on the west of South America, and [Recalde-Coronel et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1175/JHM-D-23-0064.1) (*J. Hydrometeorology*) showed that a statistically downscaled GEOS-S2S system achieved monthly precipitation skill up to 1 month mainly in **coastal lowlands and Amazon lowlands**, not the high Andes. Specifically for northern coastal Peru and Ecuador (our pilot zone: Piura, Tumbes, Lambayeque), [Montes et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2026-3371) (*EGUsphere* preprint under review at GMD, IGP RESM-COW) note that **global S2S models suffer from mean-state biases and insufficient resolution to represent Peruvian coastal upwelling and mesoscale air-sea interactions**; even their high-resolution regional coupled system (34 hindcasts, `2022-2025`, not a public operational archive) maintained coastal SST anomaly skill up to **3 weeks** while still showing reduced performance over complex Andean topography.
- **Temperature versus precipitation**: Global evaluations of GEFSv12 ([Guan et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-21-0245.1)) and SubX ([Pegion et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-18-0270.1)) confirm that 2 m temperature retains higher skill than precipitation into weeks 2 to 4 across the tropics. However, much of tropical near-surface temperature skill at weeks 2 to 4 derives from slow coastal SST and land-surface persistence, which observed weekly OISST and ERA5-Land already encode at the issue week.

### Epidemiological relevance at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks

The epidemiological literature reinforces why weather forecasts add little marginal value at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks compared with lagged observed climate:

- **Pilot evidence without weather forecasts**: [Mills and Donnelly (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0012596) (*PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases*) modeled monthly dengue incidence directly in **Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque** (`2010-2021`) using **delayed observed climate** (monthly mean maximum temperature, extreme precipitation, sustained drought, and a Peruvian coastal ENSO indicator) and produced 1-month-horizon forecasts (`2018-2021`) that classified 100% of outbreaks (DIR >= 50 or 150 per 100,000, false-alarm probabilities 0.12 and 0.05) without dynamical weather forecasts. Nationally across Peru (`2000-2018`), [Dostal, Meisner and Munayco (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0010479) likewise quantified lagged weather and ENSO (ONI and ICEN) effects on dengue outbreak risk.
- **Biological lag window**: Because *Aedes aegypti* larval maturation, adult biting survival, viral extrinsic incubation, and human intrinsic incubation together take roughly 2 to 6+ weeks, environmental anomalies that drive dengue incidence at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks have largely **already occurred by the issue week** (and for horizon 4, at most the earliest week of forecast weather overlaps the start of the biological causal window).
- **Where climate forecasts have helped dengue**: Landmark forecast-driven dengue studies such as [Lowe et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2542-5196(17)30064-5) in Machala, Ecuador (adjacent to Tumbes) and [Colón-González et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003542) in Vietnam used **seasonal climate and ENSO forecasts targeting multi-month horizons (3 to 6+ months ahead)**, well beyond this pipeline's 1-to-4-epi-week scope. Conversely, in the collaborative dengue forecasting challenge covering **Iquitos, Peru** and San Juan ([Johansson et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1909865116), *PNAS*), models incorporating complex biological mechanisms and environmental inputs had *lower* average skill than simpler statistical and multi-model ensemble approaches.

## Leakage and `available_at` discipline

If any forecast source is ever added to the panel in the future, it cannot share the same semantics as observed climate columns and must obey four strict anti-leakage rules:

1. **Separate target epi-week from `available_at`**: A forecast issued before issue week `t` for target epi-week `t + h` (`h` in 1..4) must be indexed explicitly by horizon (or stored as `(ubigeo, target_epi_year, target_epi_week, horizon, source_init_time, available_at)` or horizon-suffixed columns keyed to issue week `t`) so a modeler replaying issue week `t` filters strictly on `available_at < issue_week_start(t)`. Never store a forecast in an observed climate column for `t + h`.
2. **Stamp `available_at` at public dissemination time, never at `init_time`**:
   - **NOAA GEFSv12 operational**: `init_time + 7 hours` for days 1 to 16 (`f384`), and **`init_time + 30 hours`** (conservative ceiling over the observed `+27.5` to `+28h` S3 upload of `f840`) for days 17 to 35.
   - **NOAA CFSv2 operational (AWS)**: `init_time + 12 hours` (observed `11:16 UTC` for `00 UTC` runs).
   - **ECMWF S2S (`s2s-forecasts` on ECDS)**: **`init_time + 48 hours`** for ECMWF, NCEP, ECCC, KMA, CMA, JMA, CPTEC, CNR-ISAC, HMCR, and IAP-CAS; **`init_time + 168 hours`** (7 days) for BoM, CNRM, and UKMO.
   - **ECMWF Open Data (medium-range `<= 15 days`)**: `init_time + 10 hours` (`0p1` `360h` observed at `10:00 UTC`).
   - **CHC CHIRPS3-GEFS (`<= 16 days`)**: issue date `+ 24 hours` (exact daily publication hour is UNCONFIRMED; confirm server `Last-Modified` headers before any live use).
   - **Copernicus C3S Seasonal Forecasts**: **6th of the initialization month at `12:00 UTC`** for ECMWF SEAS5.1; **10th at `12:00 UTC`** for other C3S centres (never the 1st of the month).
   - **NOAA CPC NMME**: **9th of the initialization month at `00:00 UTC`** (conservative buffer over the `YYYYMM0800` monthly directories).
   - **IRI/CCSR ENSO Plume**: exact monthly publication date (typically 19th of the month, but can slip past the 20th, as verified on `2026-11-23`).
3. **Flag reforecasts explicitly (`is_reforecast`)**: Reforecasts (`GEFSv12 reforecast`, `CFSv2 reforecast`, `S2S reforecasts`, `SEAS5.1 hindcasts`) were not available on past issue weeks. If ever ingested for research backtests, they must carry a **synthetic `available_at`** matching their live operational release delay plus an explicit `is_reforecast = true` boolean so backtests can report operational-only and reforecast-backed results separately.
4. **No full-sample bias correction or climatology leakage**: Raw sub-seasonal models have large Andean and coastal biases, so downstream calibration (quantile matching or anomaly subtraction) is mandatory if they are used. Computing forecast anomalies or quantile maps using observed `2000-2025` means leaks future warming trends and future ENSO regimes into earlier issue weeks; any calibration must be fit strictly on years preceding each issue week (and note that CHIRPS3-GEFS's pre-computed quantile matching uses a fixed historical climatology, which violates strict expanding-window backtest purity for earlier years).

## Verdict

**Do not carry archived weather forecasts as panel columns in v1 (defer; revisit only if warranted after baseline validation).**

1. **No viable 2000 to 2026 operational archive exists at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks.** Every candidate fails at least one hard pipeline requirement: too short operationally (`2015+` ECDS S2S, `2020-09+` GEFSv12, `2026+` dynamical.org ECMWF 46-day), too short in forecast range (`<= 16 days` for ECMWF Open Data, CHIRPS3-GEFS, Open-Meteo Single Runs, AIFS, WeatherNext 2), non-commercial free API (Open-Meteo), monthly cadence (`1.0°` SEAS5.1 and NMME), or coarse resolution with disrupted archive access (CFSv2 at NCEI).
2. **Train/serve skew and imminent GEFSv13 churn outweigh expected signal.** Splicing 11-member Wednesday GEFSv12 reforecasts onto 31-member daily operational runs leaves a 9-month 2020 gap, alters ensemble variance and assimilation physics, and faces GEFSv13 replacement in Q2 FY2027.
3. **Skill over Peru does not justify the complexity at horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks.** Published S2S evaluations show rainfall tercile skill ending after week 1 across South America and poor performance over the Andes and narrow Peruvian coast, while biological dengue lags mean lagged observed ERA5-Land, CHIRPS v3.0, and weekly OISST Niño 1+2 and 3.4 already deliver the dominant climate signal for horizons 1 to 4 epi-weeks.

**Revisit gate and shortlist if ever reopened:** Only revisit weather or climate forecasts after the v1 observed-climate panel and baseline model are validated on the pilot, and only if (a) error analysis on horizons 3 and 4 shows systematic misses during rapid coastal El Niño onset (December to April in Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque), or (b) the project expands scope to horizons 5 to 12 epi-weeks. Under condition (a), run a clean **operational-only** ablation (zero reforecast skew) using `dynamical.org` **`noaa-gefs-forecast-35-day`** Zarr (`2020-10` to present, CC BY 4.0, no account) or **ECDS `s2s-forecasts` ECMWF stream** (`2015` to present, 48-hour delay, CC BY 4.0, free account), focusing first on 2 m temperature rather than raw precipitation. Under condition (b), evaluate monthly **C3S ECMWF SEAS5.1** (`1981-2026`, `1.0°`, CC BY 4.0) or **NOAA CPC NMME** (`2011-2026`, public domain) for Niño 1+2 and Niño 3.4 SST and regional temperature outlooks.
