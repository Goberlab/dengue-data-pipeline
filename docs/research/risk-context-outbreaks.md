# District-level risk context and outbreak definitions from MINSA

## Short answer

MINSA publishes no national, machine-readable table of *Aedes aegypti* presence, epidemiological scenarios (*escenarios epidemiológicos*), endemic-channel (*canal endémico*) thresholds, or dated district outbreaks. Vector presence is tracked at health-facility catchment (*localidad*) level under [NTS 198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/3957960-228-2023-minsa), with national district counts expanding from 528 in July 2022 to 609 in January 2026 ([RM 053-2026-MINSA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/7633183-053-2026-minsa)) amid incomplete surveillance. Current [NTS 230-MINSA/CDC-2025](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/6965655-473-2025-minsa) dropped the 2016 endemic-district list from repealed [NTS 125-2016](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/191438-982-2016-minsa) and leaves *canal endémico* formulas unspecified, so regional offices compute incompatible baselines. Dated outbreak labels appear only in unstructured CDC alerts, *El Peruano* emergency decrees, and weekly *Boletín Epidemiológico* PDFs, almost never with UBIGEO codes. No public MINSA source can reliably mark a district as structurally free of dengue, so the panel must retain all districts, separate `case class`, `place of infection`, `onset week`, `notification week`, `available_at` snapshots, and multi-year history.

## Aedes aegypti presence and vector surveillance

### Normative framework and spatial unit

Vector surveillance is governed by [NTS 198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/4203673/NTS%20N%C2%B0%20198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023.pdf?v=1677679701) (*Norma Técnica de Salud para la Vigilancia Entomológica y Control de Aedes aegypti, vector de Arbovirosis y la Vigilancia del Ingreso de Aedes albopictus en el territorio nacional*), approved by [RM 228-2023/MINSA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/3957960-228-2023-minsa) (signed 27 February 2023, published 1 March 2023; [RM PDF](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/4203672/Resoluci%C3%B3n%20Ministerial%20N%C2%B0%20228-2023-MINSA.pdf?v=1677679701)). Article 2 of RM 228-2023/MINSA repeals the prior vector norms RM 288-2015/MINSA (NTS 116-MINSA/DIGESA-V.01), RM 836-2020/MINSA (DS 118-MINSA/2020/DIGESA), and RVM 0010-2015-SA-DVM-SP.

DIGESA's operational unit is not the district. [NTS 198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/4203673/NTS%20N%C2%B0%20198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023.pdf?v=1677679701) (p. 3) defines *localidad* as the catchment area of a MINSA health facility (*"Jurisdicción correspondiente a un establecimiento de salud del Ministerio de Salud"*). Standard reporting forms in Anexo 06 (Formatos 01, 02, and 03, pp. 62 to 64) capture province, district name, health network, health facility (*localidad*), entomological scenario, and altitude in meters above sea level, with no UBIGEO column.

### Entomological indicators and thresholds

Section 6.2 (Tabla 1, p. 6) and Anexo 04 (p. 59) of [NTS 198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/4203673/NTS%20N%C2%B0%20198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023.pdf?v=1677679701) define five indicators:

- *Índice Aédico* (IA): `(positive dwellings / inspected dwellings) x 100`. Risk strata are low risk (*bajo riesgo*) `0 to <1%`, medium risk (*mediano riesgo*) `1 to <2%`, and high risk (*alto riesgo*) `>= 2%`.
- *Índice de Recipientes* (IR): `(positive water containers / inspected containers) x 100`.
- *Índice de Breteau* (IB): `(positive containers / inspected dwellings) x 100`.
- *Índice de Positividad de Ovitrampas* (IPO) and *Índice de Densidad de Huevos* (IDH) for ovitrap surveillance.

In pilot practice, these measurements are sparse and irregular across space and time. For example, the Subregión de Salud Luciano Castillo Colonna weekly dengue presentation for Piura ([SE 43-2025](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/regionpiura-dsrslcc/informes-publicaciones/7346865-sala-situacional-semanal-de-dengue-integral-semana-43-dia-29-de-octubre-del-2025-oficina-de-epidemiologia), pp. 24 to 28) publishes IA alongside ovitrap IPO and IDH per health facility, but the reported IA surveys come from heterogeneous past epi-weeks (ranging from epi-week 20 through epi-week 43) and many facilities have missing ovitrap readings.

CDC Perú's weekly dengue PDFs include only a static raster dispersion map with no accompanying table.

### Why no source can mark districts as structurally free of dengue

No public MINSA source can reliably classify a district as structurally free of dengue, for four reasons:

1. No national public list of vector-free (*Escenario I*) districts exists.
2. DIGESA's count of vector-positive districts is a lagged, expanding lower bound (528 to 609 districts between 2022 and 2026) produced by intermittent surveys; absence from DIGESA's list means "not detected or not surveyed," not "vector absent."
3. Altitude is only a *localidad*-level screening heuristic, not a district exclusion rule. [NTS 198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/4203673/NTS%20N%C2%B0%20198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023.pdf?v=1677679701) (Anexo 01, section 1.1, p. 20) uses altitude `<= 2,400 m` above sea level together with climate and DIGESA's ecological suitability model only to prioritize vector-negative *localidades* for surveillance. Peruvian Andean districts frequently span high-altitude peaks and warm lower valleys inside one UBIGEO, and [RM 053-2026-MINSA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/7633183-053-2026-minsa) (Plan 2026, p. 15) explicitly notes the vector's ongoing adaptation to higher-altitude ecosystems. (Peer-reviewed records in [RPMESP 2018;35(3):536-537](https://www.scielosp.org/article/rpmesp/2018.v35n3/536-537/es/) report *Ae. aegypti* in Huánuco at 2,227 m; direct page text verification is UNCONFIRMED because `scielosp.org` presented a browser challenge.)
4. Even districts truly in *Escenario I* (no vector) can legitimately record dengue notifications when residents travel to endemic areas: [NTS 230-MINSA/CDC-2025](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/8371925/6965655-nts-n-230-minsa-cdc-2025.pdf?v=1752681309) (§§6.7.18 and 6.7.19, p. 56) requires classifying every case by `place of infection` as autochthonous or imported and mandates notifying and investigating imported cases in *Escenario I* and *Escenario II*.

## Epidemiological and entomological scenarios

### Two distinct scenario schemes

MINSA currently operates two different scenario typologies under separate norms, which should not be conflated:

- **Epidemiological scenarios (*escenarios epidemiológicos*, 3 levels)** in [NTS 230-MINSA/CDC-2025](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/8371925/6965655-nts-n-230-minsa-cdc-2025.pdf?v=1752681309) (§5.1.20, pp. 4 to 5; Tabla 1, p. 8), approved by [RM 473-2025/MINSA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/6965655-473-2025-minsa) (signed 14 July 2025, published 16 July 2025; [RM PDF](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/8371924/6965655-resolucion-ministerial-n-473-2025-minsa.pdf?v=1752681308)). Article 2 of RM 473-2025/MINSA repeals [RM 982-2016/MINSA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/191438-982-2016-minsa) (NTS 125-MINSA/2016/CDC-INS), RM 734-2014/MINSA, RM 658-2010/MINSA, RVM 0026-2016-SA-DVM-SP, and RVM 014-2016-SA-DVM-SP; Article 3 repeals Anexo 04 of NTS 211-MINSA/DGIESP-2024 ([RM 175-2024/MINSA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/5323501-175-2024-minsa)). NTS 230 defines epidemiological scenarios as a dynamic stratification applicable to a district, *localidad*, or sector:
  - *Escenario I*: no vector, with or without ecological conditions for vector development, with or without imported cases, and no autochthonous cases.
  - *Escenario II*: vector present, with or without imported cases, and no autochthonous cases.
  - *Escenario III*: vector present and autochthonous cases present, subdivided into three states: *Escenario III - brote* (autochthonous cases confined to one geographic unit over a given time), *Escenario III - epidémico* (autochthonous cases across more than one geographic unit over a given time), and *Escenario III - endémico* (autochthonous cases present permanently in expected amounts).
- **Entomological scenarios (*escenarios entomológicos*, 2 levels only)** in [NTS 198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/4203673/NTS%20N%C2%B0%20198-MINSA/DIGESA-2023.pdf?v=1677679701) (§6.1, p. 5; Tabla 2, pp. 6 to 7), defined per *localidad*:
  - *Escenario Entomológico I*: *localidad* where *Ae. aegypti* or *Ae. albopictus* has never been reported, with favorable ecological conditions and introduction risk factors.
  - *Escenario Entomológico II*: *localidad* where *Ae. aegypti* has been reported, with or without arbovirus transmission. (Tabla 2 then distinguishes operational actions inside Escenario II by whether transmission is absent, endemic, or in outbreak/epidemic.) Under §6.4.1 (pp. 7 to 8), a single INS-confirmed positive dwelling upgrades a *localidad* from Escenario I to Escenario II; NTS 198 specifies no downgrade rule back to Escenario I (p. 10 specifies ovitrap verification after three consecutive 0% rounds).

### Access format and coverage

No national public table assigns Peruvian districts to *Escenarios I, II, and III* over time, in either machine-readable or PDF form. CDC alerts such as [AE-CDC 016-2023](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_202316_21_154732.pdf) (p. 3) instruct DIRESAs, GERESAs, and DIRISes to maintain local scenario tables in regional situation rooms, meaning any published scenario classifications live fragmentarily in regional PDFs rather than a national series.

## Outbreak and endemic-channel definitions

### Normative definitions of outbreak, epidemic, and closure

MINSA defines outbreaks (*brotes*) and epidemics (*epidemias*) qualitatively across two active norms:

- [NTS 230-MINSA/CDC-2025](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/8371925/6965655-nts-n-230-minsa-cdc-2025.pdf?v=1752681309):
  - *Brote* (§5.1.5, p. 3): unusual increase or appearance of cases in a delimited geographic area during a period of time affecting a given number of people (*"Incremento o aparición inusual de casos de una enfermedad en un área geográfica delimitada, durante un periodo de tiempo y que afecta a un número determinado de personas"*).
  - *Epidemia* (§5.1.19, p. 4): increase above what is normally expected in a department (*"en un departamento"*), epidemiologically linked, or simultaneous aggregation of multiple outbreaks across geographic areas that typically exceeds local service capacity. (Repealed [NTS 125-2016](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/192715/191438_RM-_N_982-2016-MINSA.pdf20180904-20266-jqjjk7.pdf?v=1593817150) §5.1.14 had defined *epidemia* as *"en una localidad o región"*.)
  - *Circulación de la enfermedad viral* (§5.1.12, p. 3): a cluster of two or more laboratory-confirmed autochthonous cases grouped in time and space.
  - Declaring authority (§§5.3 and 5.4, p. 21): CDC Perú declares *brote* nationally and *epidemia* in any jurisdiction; DIRIS, DIRESA, and GERESA epidemiology units declare *brote* in their own jurisdiction in coordination with CDC Perú.
  - Closure (*alta de brote*, §§6.2.2.8 and 6.2.2.9, pp. 38 to 39): in non-endemic zones, 48 days (two combined intrinsic and extrinsic incubation periods) since the last laboratory-positive test of the last diagnosed patient, or sustained minimal reporting over the last 48 days; in endemic zones, cases remaining continuously within historical expected values for the last 7 epi-weeks (*"de manera sostenida en las últimas 7 semanas"*). Repealed NTS 125-2016 (§6.2.2(l), p. 21) had only the 48-day rule.
- [Directiva Sanitaria 047-MINSA/DGE-V.01](https://www.dge.gob.pe/normas/D.S047%20Notificacion.pdf) (approved by [RM 545-2012/MINSA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/241888-545-2012-minsa), 28 June 2012, §§5.1.1, 5.1.2, 6.2, and 6.5.1, pp. 13 to 16): gives the same qualitative definitions of *brote* and *epidemia* and mandates immediate notification (upon awareness) into CDC's outbreak portal (`dge.gob.pe/notificar/`, now [SIEPI-Brotes](https://siepibrotes.dge.gob.pe/)).

### Surveillance and case-class regime shifts during outbreaks

Declaring an outbreak or epidemic changes how cases are tested, classified, and reported, which directly affects counts in the panel:

- **Restricted laboratory testing** ([NTS 230-MINSA/CDC-2025](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/8371925/6965655-nts-n-230-minsa-cdc-2025.pdf?v=1752681309) §6.2.2.7(f), p. 38): once a local outbreak or epidemic is confirmed, laboratory sampling is restricted to patients with warning signs, severe dengue, hospitalizations, and priority risk groups (pregnant patients, neonates, children, older adults, and patients with comorbidities).
- **Epidemiological-link confirmation (*nexo epidemiológico*)** (§5.1.33, p. 6; p. 11; §6.7.17, p. 56): enabled only during a confirmed outbreak or epidemic with proven viral circulation, for probable cases without warning signs or severity whose `place of infection` or residence lies within a 200-meter radius of a laboratory-confirmed case with symptom onset within 14 days (*"dentro de las 2 semanas"*).
- **Higher ceiling on unresolved probable cases** (§6.7.16, p. 56): only in an *epidemia* may probable cases without laboratory confirmation or epidemiological link remain classified as probable up to 20% of annual notified cases; in *brote* and endemic situations the cap is 5%.
- **Notification and febrile-surveillance shifts** (§6.2.2.3, p. 36; §6.2.2.7(h), p. 38; §6.3, p. 44): outbreaks activate daily case notification, allow regional units to suspend individual investigation forms for non-severe cases, and suspend laboratory sampling of febrile-surveillance spikes until outbreak closure.

This regime shift is visible in CDC's own alerts: at the height of the early-2024 surge ([AE-CDC 006-2024](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_20246_26_101635.pdf), epi-week 7 of 2024), 47.4% of cumulative cases (11,850 of 24,981) were still classified as probable, whereas by epi-week 46 ([AE-CDC 012-2024](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_202412_26_101656.pdf)), only 7.2% (19,696 of 273,847) remained probable after laboratory and epidemiological-link reclassification.

### Endemic-channel (*canal endémico*) methodology and divergence in practice

MINSA's arbovirus surveillance norms require *canal endémico* monitoring but prescribe no formula, reference-year count, or epidemic-year exclusion rule:

- [NTS 230-MINSA/CDC-2025](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/8371925/6965655-nts-n-230-minsa-cdc-2025.pdf?v=1752681309) (§6.8.9(a), p. 60) states: *"Análisis de tiempo para detectar o anticipar la ocurrencia de brotes. Para este análisis, se debe utilizar información de casos confirmados y probables por semana epidemiológica, a través de gráficos de línea o histogramas comparados con años previos o canal endémico."* No method, window length, or exclusion rule is given.
- Repealed [NTS 125-MINSA/2016/CDC-INS](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/192715/191438_RM-_N_982-2016-MINSA.pdf20180904-20266-jqjjk7.pdf?v=1593817150) (§6.9.1, p. 30) similarly stated only that situation rooms in endemic zones must include a *canal endémico*, with no formula.

The methodological reference hosted directly by CDC Perú is PAHO's *Módulos de Principios de Epidemiología para el Control de Enfermedades (MOPECE)*, 3rd edition, Module 4 ([dge.gob.pe hosted PDF](https://www.dge.gob.pe/material-interes/CANCER_Modulo-de-Principios-de-Epidemiologia.pdf), pp. 34 to 37, PDF pp. 47 to 50). MOPECE cites Bortman (1999) when introducing the *corredor* or *canal endémico* and specifies the quartile/median procedure verbatim:

- **Reference years**: *"Para construir el corredor endémico de una enfermedad se requiere contar con las frecuencias semanales o mensuales que son correspondientes a una serie de siete o más años... En caso de no contar con información de los siete años, puede hacerse con cinco o tres años."* (p. 36).
- **Exclusions**: *"En caso de tener años epidémicos, estos deben excluirse."* (p. 36).
- **Unit**: weekly or monthly (*semanas o meses*), using either case counts or incidence rates (*"tanto con casos, como con tasas de enfermedad"*, p. 36; recommending rates when comparing areas of different population size, p. 37).
- **Curves and zones**: lower quartile (`q1`, 25%), median (`q2`, 50%), and upper quartile (`q3`, 75%, called the *umbral epidémico*), defining four zones: *zona de éxito* (`0` to `q1`), *zona de seguridad* (`q1` to `q2`), *zona de alarma o alerta* (`q2` to `q3`), and *zona de epidemia* (above `q3`).

In live operations, CDC Perú and regional health authorities do not follow one uniform dengue *canal endémico*:

- **CDC national dengue dashboards and reports**: Neither CDC's daily dengue dashboard ([sala_diaria_dengue.html](https://app7.dge.gob.pe/maps2/sala_diaria_dengue.html)) nor CDC's national weekly dengue PDFs ([SE 52-2022](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/dengue/dengue_202252_09_234420.pdf), [SE 52-2023](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/dengue/dengue_202352_11_130418.pdf), [SE 19-2024](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/dengue-semanal/dengue-semanal_202419_20_110941.pdf)) publish a dengue *canal endémico*; they plot current epi-week case counts against prior individual years.
- **CDC non-dengue dashboards (for comparison)**: CDC's acute diarrheal disease ([shiny_eda_web](https://app7.dge.gob.pe/maps2/shiny_eda_web/)) and acute respiratory infection ([shiny_ira_web](https://app7.dge.gob.pe/maps2/shiny_ira_web/)) dashboards do publish *canales endémicos*, with the explicit note: *"El canal endémico solo está a nivel nacional y por departamento. No se consideran los años pandémicos en el cálculo (2020 al 2022)."* (No district-level channels are published there.)
- **Regional dengue implementations diverge**:
  - **Subregión de Salud Luciano Castillo Colonna (Piura pilot)** ([SE 43-2025](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/regionpiura-dsrslcc/informes-publicaciones/7346865-sala-situacional-semanal-de-dengue-integral-semana-43-dia-29-de-octubre-del-2025-oficina-de-epidemiologia), p. 4) publishes a sub-regional dengue *canal endémico* across epi-weeks 1 to 52 in case counts with four zones (*Éxito, Seguridad, Alarma, Epidemia*), without stating the formula, reference years, case-class filter, or epidemic-year exclusions.
  - **Red de Salud La Convención (Cusco)** ([SE 32-2026](https://redsaludlaconvencion.gob.pe/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sala-situacional-de-dengue-2026-actualizado-al-15-08-2026.pdf), p. 9) publishes a district-level dengue *canal endémico* (Santa Ana) with verbatim footnotes: *"Nota 1: Canal endémico elaborado mediante el método de Bortman con información histórica de casos confirmados de dengue correspondiente al periodo 2020-2025. Nota 2: Tomar en cuenta que el presente gráfico del canal endémico está construido con semanas epidemiológicas completas, se excluye la última semana en curso."* This implementation uses **confirmed cases only** (diverging from NTS 230 §6.8.9(a)), uses 6 reference years **including** the 2020 to 2022 pandemic years and 2023 to 2024 national epidemic years, excludes the incomplete current epi-week, and labels the curves `Q1`, `Q2`, and `Q3` despite citing Bortman (which uses geometric means and confidence intervals rather than quartiles).
  - **DIRESA Amazonas** ([CDC Friday teleconference SE 26-2021](https://www.dge.gob.pe/portal/docs/tools/teleconferencia/2021/SE262021/04.pdf), p. 5) presented a departmental dengue *canal endémico* built on an 11-year reference period (`2010-2021*`), with no stated exclusions.

Access format and coverage: there is no public national dengue *canal endémico* table, machine-readable or PDF. Regional channels exist only as charts (occasionally with small weekly cutoff tables) inside regional PDF decks.

## Dated outbreak labels

Three public MINSA channels provide dated outbreak markers, none of which is a clean, national, machine-readable district × epi-week label table.

### CDC Perú epidemiological alerts (*alertas epidemiológicas*)

- **Access format and coverage**: Indexed publicly at [dge.gob.pe](https://www.dge.gob.pe/portalnuevo/informacion-publica/avisos-y-alertas-epidemiologicas/), which embeds [epipublic.dge.gob.pe category 7](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/publico/listado/7/2026/0) (*Alertas*; category 24 holds *Avisos*). The HTML listing is backed by a public POST endpoint (`https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/publico/listado_ajax`, parameters `bq_anio` and `bq_categoria=7`) that spans 1993 to 2026 (irregular in early years, continuous from 2014 to 2026). From 2019 through 2026, 21 dengue-related national alerts were identified (2019: 3; 2020: 3; 2021: 3; 2022: 3; 2023: 5; 2024: 3; 2025: 0; 2026: 1). Regional DIRESAs also issue local alerts under NTS 230 §6.2.2.6(a), scattered across regional websites.
- **Granularity and dating**: Every alert is an unstructured PDF carrying an explicit issue date, digital signature dates, and a surveillance cutoff epi-week. Some alerts name individual districts in prose, without UBIGEO codes, while others stay at national or departmental level:
  - [AE 003-2023](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_20233_09_113027.pdf) (dated 8 February 2023, signed 9 February 2023, cutoff SE 05-2023): national alert citing 528 vector-positive districts.
  - [AE-CDC 016-2023](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_202316_21_154732.pdf) (dated 18 August 2023, signed 21 August 2023): reports 91 districts in outbreak (*situación de brote*), including 40 new outbreak districts not covered by emergency decree DS 009-2023-SA, giving counts by department rather than a full district roster.
  - [AE-CDC 006-2024](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_20246_26_101635.pdf) (dated 24 February 2024, signed 26 February 2024, cutoff SE 07-2024): national epidemic alert citing 476 districts reporting cases, 261 in active transmission, and 67 on a plateau (*meseta*), without naming them.
  - [AE-CDC 012-2024](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_202412_26_101656.pdf) (dated and signed 22 November 2024, cutoff SE 46-2024): names 31 districts in prose as being in *situación de brote* (including pilot districts Castilla, Paita, Piura, Sullana, Pariñas, Chulucanas, and Tumbes), alongside counts of 52 active-transmission and 68 plateau districts.
  - [AE-CDC 001-2026](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/alertas/alertas_20261_09_145341.pdf) (dated 9 January 2026, signed 12 January 2026, cutoff SE 52-2025): names specific districts in prose showing an upward notification trend over the previous six epi-weeks.

### SIEPI-Brotes and weekly Boletín Epidemiológico outbreak tables

Under [Directiva Sanitaria 047-MINSA/DGE-V.01](https://www.dge.gob.pe/normas/D.S047%20Notificacion.pdf) (§6.5.1, p. 16) and [NTS 230-MINSA/CDC-2025](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/8371925/6965655-nts-n-230-minsa-cdc-2025.pdf?v=1752681309) (§6.2.2.2, p. 36), every suspected or confirmed outbreak is logged in [SIEPI-Brotes](https://siepibrotes.dge.gob.pe/). The application itself requires user credentials and has no public data export.

However, CDC Perú publishes a weekly public extract titled *"Brotes y otros EVISAP en Perú"* inside each weekly [Boletín Epidemiológico del Perú](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/publico/listado/1/2026/0) PDF. In Vol. 35, SE 37-2026 ([boletin_202637_20_145551.pdf](https://epipublic.dge.gob.pe/uploads/boletin/boletin_202637_20_145551.pdf), p. 1027, PDF p. 19, sourced from `SIEpi-Brotes` through 17 September 2026), the table reports:

- `Fecha de conocimiento` (date health authorities learned of the event)
- `Fecha de notificación` (date logged in SIEPI-Brotes)
- `Lugar` (department, province, district, and *localidad* names, with no UBIGEO)
- `Brote o EVISAP` (e.g., Dengue)
- `N° de casos`, `Laboratorio` (e.g., `NS1 (+)`, `IgM (+)`), and `Riesgo de transmisión` (`Alto` or `Medio`)

That single issue lists pilot-region dengue outbreaks in Lambayeque (José Leonardo Ortiz, Chiclayo, Pimentel) and Piura (Marcavelica), showing that SIEPI-Brotes does hold dated district-level outbreak events. As published, however, the weekly bulletin tables are PDF-only, list only newly reported events for that epi-week without closure dates, omit UBIGEO codes, and contain manual spelling errors (for example, `Izarola` for Irazola on p. 1027).

## What the panel must make computable

Because MINSA neither publishes a machine-readable risk stratification nor enforces a single *canal endémico* formula or district outbreak label, the panel should not hard-code one outbreak threshold or filter out districts up front. Instead, to support downstream decisions in issues `#13` and `#14`, the panel must preserve the raw ingredients that let modelers compute any MINSA-compatible risk stratum or outbreak definition without lookahead:

1. **Separate `case class` counts (`confirmed` and `probable`)**: NTS 230 §6.8.9(a) uses confirmed plus probable cases for time analysis, while some regional *canales endémicos* (such as Red La Convención 2026) use confirmed cases only. Keeping both classes separate also lets downstream consumers observe the surveillance regime shift during outbreaks and epidemics, when laboratory testing narrows (§6.2.2.7(f)) and the allowable probable share rises from 5% to 20% (§6.7.16), or spikes temporarily near 50% in real time before reclassification (as in AE-CDC 006-2024).
2. **Both `onset week` and `notification week`, stamped with `available_at` snapshots**: computing real-time *canales endémicos* requires excluding incomplete current epi-weeks (as Red La Convención explicitly does) and evaluating historical thresholds using only data published before the `issue week`. Snapshot history is also needed to reproduce outbreak closure windows (48 days in non-endemic zones, 7 epi-weeks in endemic zones) and to avoid lookahead bias from retrospective probable-to-confirmed or discarded reclassifications.
3. **`Place of infection` distinct from patient residence (and autochthonous versus imported status where published)**: distinguishing *Escenario I* and *Escenario II* imported notifications from *Escenario III* local transmission (§5.1.20 and §§6.7.18 to 6.7.19 of NTS 230) depends on `place of infection`. If a source mixes residence and `place of infection`, imported cases in vector-free districts can falsely look like endemic transmission.
4. **Long historical depth (at least 7 to 10 epi-years) plus district population denominators**: MOPECE's standard *canal endémico* requires 7 or more non-epidemic years (with fallbacks to 5 or 3). Because Peru experienced major dengue epidemics in 2017, 2023, and 2024 plus COVID-19 surveillance disruption in 2020 to 2022 (excluded on CDC's EDA and IRA dashboards), a modeler excluding epidemic or pandemic years needs a decade or more of history to retain 5 to 7 baseline years. Storing district population denominators alongside counts supports both case-count channels and rate-based channels (as MOPECE recommends for comparing districts of different sizes).
5. **Full national district frame with `missing != zero`**: because no public source marks districts as structurally free of dengue, the panel must not drop districts absent from DIGESA's vector count or above 2,400 m elevation, nor impute zero from non-listing or unnotified epi-weeks. Any geographic subsetting for modeling belongs downstream of the panel and should be explicit and reversible.

## Open questions

- **Transparency-request candidates (for `#21`)**: Three unpublished internal MINSA records would directly close the biggest gaps here if requested under Peru's transparency law (Ley 27806): (1) DIGESA's historical district and *localidad* *Aedes aegypti* presence and IA database with UBIGEO codes and survey dates; (2) CDC Perú's historical SIEPI-Brotes dengue outbreak log with UBIGEO, `Fecha de conocimiento`, `Fecha de notificación`, and outbreak closure date (*alta de brote*); and (3) CDC Perú's internal technical specification (if any) for dengue *canales endémicos* and district *escenarios epidemiológicos*.
- **Blocked portals requiring manual browser verification**: `digesa.minsa.gob.pe` (Cloudflare HTTP 403) and the `datosabiertos.gob.pe` CKAN API (CloudWAF) could not be audited programmatically. A human check from a standard browser is needed to verify whether DIGESA's vector pages currently host downloadable spreadsheets or dated district PDFs, and whether any DIGESA entomological resource exists on `datosabiertos.gob.pe`.
- **Optional auxiliary metadata from decrees and bulletins**: Before deciding whether to parse non-machine-readable outbreak labels for model evaluation, downstream design should decide whether extracting (a) the 6-digit UBIGEO budget table in scanned [DS 004-2024-SA](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/normas-legales/5274350-004-2024-sa) Annex I Table 3 or (b) the weekly *Boletín Epidemiológico* "Brotes y otros EVISAP" PDF tables is worth the OCR and name-matching effort given their administrative lag and incomplete coverage.
- **Pilot-region situation rooms**: This review verified one Piura sub-regional situation room ([SRSLCC SE 43-2025](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/regionpiura-dsrslcc/informes-publicaciones/7346865-sala-situacional-semanal-de-dengue-integral-semana-43-dia-29-de-octubre-del-2025-oficina-de-epidemiologia)) as an operational illustration; whether DIRESA Tumbes, DIRESA Piura, and GERESA Lambayeque publish consistent district scenario tables or local alerts should be reconciled with the pilot regional sources audit.
