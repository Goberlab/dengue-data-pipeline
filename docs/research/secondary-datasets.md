# Secondary datasets covering dengue in Peru

## Short answer

No independent public secondary dataset covers all Peruvian districts by epi-week, so none can fill our national historical or live gap or supply reliable `available_at` publication history. Every national subnational secondary source we found (OpenDengue, Mills et al., `denguedatasets`, and recent Zenodo, Harvard Dataverse, and GitHub releases) derives from Peru's Ministry of Health (MINSA) CDC Perú surveillance database (`datosabiertos.gob.pe` or the `dge.gob.pe` Sala Situacional). OpenDengue stops at Admin2 (provinces, 2000 to 2023), PAHO PLISA and WHO Global Dengue stop at national totals for Peru (PAHO ARBO Portal Admin1 availability is UNCONFIRMED), and Mills et al. (`peru_dengue_province`) ships a May 2023 (epi-week 19) line-list snapshot from CDC Perú covering 2010 to 2023 W19 without UBIGEO codes. These sources serve only to validate counts and check retrospective revisions, not to feed the panel.

## Dataset details

### OpenDengue (v1.3.1)

OpenDengue is maintained by the Dengue Mapping and Modelling Group at LSHTM ([opendengue.org](https://opendengue.org/data.html), [Clarke et al. 2024, Scientific Data](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03120-7)). The latest release is `v1.3.1`, published on 2026-11-03 on [GitHub](https://github.com/OpenDengue/master-repo/releases/tag/v1.3.1) and [Figshare v5](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24259573.v5). GitHub release `v1.3.1` carries no release assets; the extract ZIPs live in [`data/releases/V1.3.1/`](https://github.com/OpenDengue/master-repo/tree/v1.3.1/data/releases/V1.3.1) and match Figshare's MD5 checksums byte-for-byte.

We downloaded and profiled all three v1.3.1 extracts (`National_extract_V1_3_1.zip`, `Spatial_extract_V1_3_1.zip`, and `Temporal_extract_V1_3_1.zip`) and matched every Peru `UUID` against [`sourcedata_V1.3.1.csv`](https://github.com/OpenDengue/master-repo/blob/v1.3.1/data/raw_data/sourcedata_V1.3.1.csv).

- **Peru geography**: OpenDengue stops at **Admin2 (provinces)**, mapped to FAO GAUL codes (`FAO_GAUL_code`) and Natural Earth codes (`RNE_iso_code`). It has **zero district (Admin3) rows** and no UBIGEO codes. Across `Spatial_extract_V1_3_1.zip` (30,782 Peru rows) and `Temporal_extract_V1_3_1.zip` (37,263 Peru rows), Peru has 115 unique provinces across 22 departments at Admin2 (including all 14 provinces in the pilot: Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque), plus 18 to 25 departments at Admin1 in selected years, and Admin0 national rows.
- **Temporal resolution and years**:
  - `Admin2` by epi-week (`T_res = Week`): 30,685 rows covering `2000-01-02` to `2023-12-30` (24 years, 757,350 total cases, `UUID = MOH-PER-20002023-Y02-00`), plus 306 extra Admin2 weekly rows in `Temporal_extract` for three Cajamarca provinces (`CHOTA`, `CUTERVO`, `JAEN`) in 2007 to 2008 (`MOH-PER-2007-W01` and `MOH-PER-2008-W01`). There is no subnational data for 2024 or later.
  - `Admin1` by epi-week (`Temporal_extract` only): 2007 to 2008 (25 departments) and 2011 to 2013 (18 to 25 departments) from `MOH-PER-2007-W01`, `MOH-PER-2008-W01`, `MOH-PER-2011-Y01-02`, `MOH-PER-2012-Y01-03`, and `MOH-PER-2013-Y01-04`.
  - `Admin0` by epi-week and annual: annual counts 1980 to 2024 (`WHOPAHO-ALL-19802024-Y03-00` and `Y03-01`) and weekly counts 2014 to 2024 (`WHOPAHO-ALL-20142022-W01` and `WHOPAHO-ALL-20222024-W02`). In `National_extract_V1_3_1.zip` (812 Peru rows), OpenDengue stitches together `MOH-PER` weekly sums (2000, 2001, 2007 to 2009, 2011 to 2013), PAHO PLISA weekly counts (2016 to 2019, 2021 to 2023, with several 2016, 2018, and 2019 weeks marked `(Imputed)`), and PAHO PLISA annual counts (1980 to 1999, 2002 to 2006, 2010, 2014, 2015, 2020, 2024).
- **Provenance**: According to [`sourcedata_V1.3.1.csv`](https://github.com/OpenDengue/master-repo/blob/v1.3.1/data/raw_data/sourcedata_V1.3.1.csv), the entire Admin2 2000 to 2023 series (`MOH-PER-20002023-Y02-00`, standardized as `Probable and confirmed`) was aggregated from Peru's national open data line-list at [`https://www.datosabiertos.gob.pe/dataset/vigilancia-epidemiol%C3%B3gica-de-dengue`](https://www.datosabiertos.gob.pe/dataset/vigilancia-epidemiol%C3%B3gica-de-dengue). The 2007 to 2008 and 2011 to 2013 Admin1/Admin2 weekly series came from CDC Perú weekly situation rooms ([`https://www.dge.gob.pe/portalnuevo/informacion-publica/situacion-del-dengue-en-el-peru/`](https://www.dge.gob.pe/portalnuevo/informacion-publica/situacion-del-dengue-en-el-peru/)). The Admin0 1980 to 2024 series came from PAHO PLISA ([`https://www3.paho.org/data/index.php/en/mnu-topics/indicadores-dengue-en/dengue-nacional-en/252-dengue-pais-ano-en.html`](https://www3.paho.org/data/index.php/en/mnu-topics/indicadores-dengue-en/dengue-nacional-en/252-dengue-pais-ano-en.html), case definition `Suspected + probable + confirmed, non-severe & severe`, standardized as `Total`). Note that OpenDengue's `Year` column records the calendar year of `calendar_start_date`, which shifts boundary epi-weeks (e.g., 2014 W1 starting `2013-12-29`) into the prior calendar year.
- **License**: The Figshare dataset ([`10.6084/m9.figshare.24259573.v5`](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24259573.v5)), source archive ([`10.6084/m9.figshare.24468397.v6`](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24468397.v6)), website footer ([opendengue.org/data.html](https://opendengue.org/data.html)), and *Scientific Data* paper ([`10.1038/s41597-024-03120-7`](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03120-7)) all specify **CC BY 4.0**. However, the older 2023 JSON-LD metadata files shipped on Figshare and GitHub (`metadata_main.json` and `metadata_source.json`) still declare **CC BY-SA 4.0**, and [`OpenDengue/master-repo`](https://github.com/OpenDengue/master-repo) has no `LICENSE` file.
- **Use for us**: **Validate only.** It cannot fill any district-level or post-2023 gap because it aggregates our own primary `datosabiertos.gob.pe` file up to provinces, stops at 2023 for subnational data, and does not carry historical `available_at` publication times. Its `Admin2` totals (2000 to 2023) and `Admin0` PAHO totals (1980 to 2024) are useful for sanity-checking province and national aggregations.

### PAHO PLISA and PAHO ARBO Portal

PAHO previously served dengue indicators on the legacy PLISA Joomla portal (`https://www3.paho.org/data/index.php/en/mnu-topics/indicadores-dengue-en.html`). As of 2026-11-22, those `www3.paho.org` endpoints return **HTTP 502 Bad Gateway**, and `https://www.paho.org/plisa` permanently redirects (`301`) to [`https://opendata.paho.org/en`](https://opendata.paho.org/en) (Core Indicators). PAHO's live dengue dashboards now reside on the **PAHO ARBO Portal** ([`https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/dengue-data-and-analysis`](https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/dengue-data-and-analysis), country analysis at [`https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/dengue-data-and-analysis/dengue-analysis-country`](https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/dengue-data-and-analysis/dengue-analysis-country), and Peru country profile at [`https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/countries/peru-country-profile`](https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/countries/peru-country-profile)).

- **Peru geography**:
  - **National (Admin0)** is confirmed in both legacy PLISA and the live ARBO Portal (`1008en_NAC_Indicadores_reporte_semanal.asp` embedded on the country analysis page, verified via the active Selenium scraper in [`DengueGlobalObservatory/PAHO-crawler`](https://github.com/DengueGlobalObservatory/PAHO-crawler/blob/main/scripts/PAHOCrawler_UCver_v5.py) and its downloaded CSVs).
  - **Legacy PLISA subnational pages did NOT cover Peru.** The 2024-03-05 Wayback Machine snapshot of [`dengue-subnacional-en.html`](https://web.archive.org/web/20240305114012/https://www3.paho.org/data/index.php/en/mnu-topics/indicadores-dengue-en/dengue-subnacional-en.html) lists only eight countries (Bolivia, Costa Rica, Ecuador, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, and Venezuela), matching both OpenDengue's *Scientific Data* paper ([`10.1038/s41597-024-03120-7`](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03120-7)) and [`sourcedata_V1.3.1.csv`](https://github.com/OpenDengue/master-repo/blob/v1.3.1/data/raw_data/sourcedata_V1.3.1.csv), which hold zero PAHO subnational rows for Peru.
  - **Live ARBO Portal Peru country profile**: [`peru-country-profile`](https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/countries/peru-country-profile) embeds [`https://ais.paho.org/ArboPortal/DENG/1024_DENG_PER_EN_Indicadores.asp`](https://ais.paho.org/ArboPortal/DENG/1024_DENG_PER_EN_Indicadores.asp), which loads a Tableau workbook (`phip.paho.org/trusted/.../views/1024en/Cases`). Direct non-browser HTTP fetches to `phip.paho.org` return `403 Forbidden`, so whether `1024en/Cases` currently exposes downloadable Admin1 (department) tables for Peru is **UNCONFIRMED**. Neither PAHO PLISA nor the ARBO Portal publishes Peru data at province (Admin2) or district (Admin3) resolution.
- **Time resolution and years**: Annual national totals from **1980 to present** and cumulative year-to-date counts by **epi-week** from **2014 to present** (46 countries and territories report weekly; verified in [`PAHO-crawler`](https://github.com/DengueGlobalObservatory/PAHO-crawler/tree/main/data/OD_DL_20250513) CSVs and OpenDengue `WHOPAHO` rows). Because PAHO reports cumulative counts, converting to weekly incident cases requires differencing across epi-weeks and can produce negative values or revisions when countries back-adjust totals.
- **Provenance**: Weekly reports submitted by MINSA / CDC Perú (and national bulletins) to PAHO ([ARBO Portal](https://www.paho.org/en/arbo-portal/dengue-data-and-analysis)). PAHO's `Total of Dengue Cases` column includes suspected, probable, and confirmed cases (`Total`), which systematically exceeds `datosabiertos.gob.pe` confirmed-plus-probable totals (for example, in 2023 PAHO reports 274,227 total cases with 230,872 laboratory-confirmed, whereas `datosabiertos.gob.pe` via OpenDengue Admin2 sums to 256,641 probable and confirmed).
- **Access**: Interactive Tableau dashboards via `ais.paho.org` and `phip.paho.org`; no documented public REST API. Automated retrieval requires headless browser automation (as done in [`DengueGlobalObservatory/PAHO-crawler`](https://github.com/DengueGlobalObservatory/PAHO-crawler)).
- **License**: [`opendata.paho.org/en/about`](https://opendata.paho.org/en/about) places PLISA under **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 3.0 IGO (CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO)** unless otherwise stated. General PAHO site use is also governed by [PAHO Terms and Conditions](https://www.paho.org/en/terms-and-conditions-use-site).
- **Use for us**: **Validate only** (coarse national ceiling check and serotype metadata). It has no district or province series for Peru, mixes suspected cases into total counts, has no direct API, and carries a non-commercial license.

### WHO Global Dengue Surveillance

WHO maintains the [Global Dengue Surveillance dashboard](https://worldhealthorg.shinyapps.io/dengue_global/) backed by a public OData API discovered in the app HTML: [`https://xmart-api-public.who.int/ARBOV/V_DENGUE_GLOBAL_VALIDATED_PUBLIC`](https://xmart-api-public.who.int/ARBOV/V_DENGUE_GLOBAL_VALIDATED_PUBLIC).

- **Peru geography, time resolution, and years**: **Admin0 (national) only.** Directly querying the live OData API (`$filter=ISO3 eq 'PER'`) returns **662 non-null epi-week rows** (`DATE_TYPE = epiweek`) spanning **2014 W1 (`2013-12-29`) through 2026 W37 (`2026-09-13`)**, with fields `CASES`, `CONFIRMED_CASES`, `SEVERE_CASES`, `DEATHS`, and `SERO_1` to `SERO_4`. By contrast, the Excel workbook exported from the Shiny dashboard (verified via the 2026-09-18 archive in [`DengueGlobalObservatory/WHOGlobal-crawler`](https://github.com/DengueGlobalObservatory/WHOGlobal-crawler/blob/main/Downloads/dengue-global-data-2026-09-18.xlsx)) aggregates Peru to **152 monthly rows** (`Jan 2014` to `Aug 2026`).
- **Provenance**: National Member State reports routed through PAHO/AMRO; weekly counts match PAHO PLISA (e.g., 2014 W1 is 148 cases in both WHO xmart and OpenDengue's PAHO PLISA extract).
- **License**: Governed by [WHO Terms and Conditions of Use for WHO Data Compilations](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/data-policy/terms-and-conditions) (non-commercial, revocable, prohibits redistribution outside licensed use).
- **Use for us**: **Validate only.** For national epi-week cross-checks in 2024 to 2026, WHO's public OData API is far cleaner to query programmatically than PAHO's Tableau embeds, though it remains Admin0 only and carries WHO's restrictive data terms.

### Mills et al. (2024 and 2025)

Two papers and two GitHub repositories by Cathal Mills and colleagues cover dengue modeling in the three pilot departments (Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque):

1. **Mills & Donnelly (2024)**, "Climate-based modelling and forecasting of dengue in three endemic departments of Peru", *PLoS Neglected Tropical Diseases* 18(12):e0012596 ([`10.1371/journal.pntd.0012596`](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0012596), CC BY 4.0) and repo [`cathalmills/peru_dengue`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue) (pushed 2023-08-16, no `LICENSE` file).
   - **What ships in the repo**: Six R files and a 122-byte README only; **no data files ship**. [`data_processing.R`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue/blob/main/data_processing.R) reads a local unshipped CSV named `2010_2019_cases_full_data.csv` (which actually contained 2023 rows per line 111 comment `#360 observations in 2023 without pop estimate`), groups by `Departamnento`, `Semana`, and `Ano`, and aggregates to department-month totals for 2010 to 2021. The paper's reference 19 cites `CDC Peru. Reporte de Tabla de casos notificados por causas; 2023` at `https://www.dge.gob.pe/salasituacional/`.
2. **Mills, Falconi-Agapito, Carrera, Munayco, Kraemer, & Donnelly (2025)**, "Multi-model approach to understand and predict past and future dengue epidemic dynamics", *Royal Society Open Science* 12(11):241870 ([`10.1098/rsos.241870`](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.241870), published 2025-11-05, CC BY 4.0), repo [`cathalmills/peru_dengue_province`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue_province) (no `LICENSE` file), Zenodo archive of tag `pre-publication` ([`10.5281/zenodo.17042126`](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17042126), published 2025-09-02, CC BY 4.0), and Figshare supplementary material ([`10.6084/m9.figshare.c.8079963`](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.8079963), containing a single PDF `rsos241870_si_001.pdf` at [Figshare article 30317784](https://rs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Supplementary_Information_SI_from_Multi-model_approach_to_understand_and_predict_past_and_future_dengue_epidemic_dynamics/30317784), CC BY 4.0, no data files).

#### What the Mills et al. case data actually is

`cathalmills/peru_dengue_province` ships [`data/cases/2010_2021_cases_full_data.csv`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue_province/blob/main/data/cases/2010_2021_cases_full_data.csv) (33,757,767 bytes, git blob SHA `d278d0afc4a81222d9600e4236dd48c412a15062`, committed 2025-01-30 in `e1435405`, identical in the `pre-publication` tag archived on Zenodo). Direct profiling of the full file shows:

- **Unit and schema**: **492,597 individual case rows** (one row per case, `DIS = 1` on every row, `Eventos o daños = Dengue` on every row). The 10 columns are `Departamnento` (sic), `Provincia`, `Distrito`, `Ano`, `Semana actualización`, `Eventos o daños`, `Semana`, `Sexo`, `Tipo de diagnóstico`, and `DIS`. There is **no UBIGEO code** and no exact date.
- **Years and snapshot**: Despite the filename `2010_2021_cases_full_data.csv`, the file spans **2010 epi-week 1 through 2023 epi-week 19** (14 years; 2014 and 2020 include epi-week 53 with 232 and 1,184 rows nationally, including 60 and 23 pilot rows). Every row has `Semana actualización = 19`, proving it is a single **snapshot** pulled around mid-May 2023.
- **Geography**: **22 non-empty departments** and **615 non-empty department/province/district triples** (of which **110 triples** fall in the 14 provinces of the pilot: Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque). Exactly **354 rows** have empty department, province, and district simultaneously (5 in 2021, 194 in 2022, 155 in 2023); no rows have partially missing geography.
- **Case class**: `Confirmados` = 409,200 (83.1%) and `Probables` = 83,397 (16.9%). Probable cases are virtually absent before 2015 (0 in 2010 and 2011, 1 in 2012, 3 in 2013, 12 in 2014, then 3,327 in 2015 and 41,791 of 83,693 rows in 2023 W1 to W19).
- **Source provenance and empirical reconciliation**: Both papers cite CDC Perú's Sala Situacional ([`https://www.dge.gob.pe/salasituacional/`](https://www.dge.gob.pe/salasituacional/), `Reporte de tabla de casos notificados por causas`; README also links [`https://www.dge.gob.pe/sala-situacional-dengue/#grafico01`](https://www.dge.gob.pe/sala-situacional-dengue/#grafico01)). Neither the CSV nor the papers document whether `Semana` is onset week or notification week, or whether `Distrito` is place of infection or residence; both remain formally **UNCONFIRMED**. However, our empirical province-by-epi-week reconciliation against OpenDengue v1.3.1 `Admin2` (`MOH-PER-20002023-Y02-00`, aggregated directly from `datosabiertos.gob.pe`) reveals a near-exact match:
  - Across **2010 to 2021 in the 14 pilot provinces**, Mills sums to **120,219** cases vs OpenDengue's **120,218** (a difference of 1 case in 12 years). Nationally across 2010 to 2022, Mills has 408,904 cases vs OpenDengue's 408,947 (0.01% difference).
  - Week-by-week across all 14 pilot provinces, **448 of 448** province-week cells in 2016 (`10,361` cases) and **455 of 455** cells in 2021 (`6,370` cases) match with **zero difference** (`max_abs_diff = 0`). During the 2017 Piura province El Niño peak (weeks 10 to 26), all 17 consecutive weeks match to the exact integer (52 of 53 weeks exact in 2017; the single-week annual shifts in 2012 to 2014 and 2017 to 2020 come from OpenDengue assigning epi-weeks to calendar year via `calendar_start_date`).
  - This proves Mills's CSV is downstream of the same CDC Perú NOTI-SP confirmed-plus-probable database as `datosabiertos.gob.pe`, with `Semana` aligned to `datosabiertos.gob.pe`'s epi-week (zero reporting-delay lag at province-week level).
- **Processing quirks in `scripts/processing/case_data.R`**: Although the raw CSV has districts and epi-weeks, [`scripts/processing/case_data.R`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue_province/blob/main/scripts/processing/case_data.R) immediately collapses counts to **province by epi-week**, drops blank provinces, cross-joins only `weeks = 1:52` with a left merge (`all.x = TRUE`, silently dropping epi-week 53 in 2014 and 2020: 60 and 23 pilot cases), converts epi-week numbers to dates via `ISOweek::ISOweek2date` (Monday-based ISO weeks rather than MINSA's Sunday to Saturday epi-weeks), sums into **monthly** counts for the 14 pilot provinces, and truncates to **May 2010 to December 2021**.

#### Climate, population, and boundary data used by Mills et al.

Verified directly from [`README.md`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue_province/blob/main/README.md), [`scripts/processing/climate.R`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue_province/blob/main/scripts/processing/climate.R), and the `workflows/datasets/` Snakefiles in `cathalmills/peru_dengue_province` (none of the climate files ship in the repo; Snakemake downloads them):

- **Temperature and precipitation**: WorldClim 2.1 historical monthly weather (`tmin`, `tmax`, `prec`) at 2.5 arc-minute resolution (note: the 2024 paper text mentions 1 km, but the Snakefile [`workflows/datasets/worldclim/workflow/Snakefile`](https://github.com/cathalmills/peru_dengue_province/blob/main/workflows/datasets/worldclim/workflow/Snakefile) downloads `2.5m` CRU-TS 4.06 downscaled rasters for 2010 to 2021 from `geodata.ucdavis.edu/climate/worldclim/2_1/hist/cts4.06/2.5m/`), averaged spatially over each province. WorldClim 2.1 monthly weather is unsuitable for live weekly forecasting (monthly resolution and multi-year publication lag).
- **Drought (SPI-6)**: European Drought Observatory / Copernicus Global Drought Observatory GPCC SPI-6 monthly NetCDF rasters (2001 to 2023) from [`drought.emergency.copernicus.eu`](https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/dataset/1534c8f7-42e8-4212-b2dd-6388f60987eb#dataaccess) (`workflows/datasets/spi6/workflow/Snakefile`).
- **ENSO indices**: Monthly NOAA CPC Oceanic Niño Index / detrended Niño 3.4 ([`detrend.nino34.ascii.txt`](http://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/ensostuff/detrend.nino34.ascii.txt)) and IGP Indice Costero El Niño ([`met.igp.gob.pe/datos/icen.txt`](http://met.igp.gob.pe/datos/icen.txt)).
- **Population and urbanity (shipped in repo)**: `data/province/province_pop.csv` (1993, 2007, 2017 census + 2022 estimates), `data/province/2007_census_data.xlsx`, `data/province/census_data.xlsx` (INEI 2007 and 2017 censuses), plus INEI `proy_04.xls` downloaded by Snakemake.
- **Administrative boundaries**: IGN 2020 Admin2 shapefiles (`per_adm_ign_20200714_shp.zip`) from [HDX COD-AB Peru](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/cod-ab-per).
- **Use for us**: **Validate only.** Reconcile our pilot province and district aggregations against Mills's CSV (accounting for their week-53 drop and `ISOweek2date` month mapping when comparing against their published monthly series), and optionally use `Semana actualización = 19` (May 2023) as a single historical snapshot with approximate `available_at` in mid-May 2023 to inspect retrospective revisions in 2022 and early 2023. Do not use it to fill data gaps because it lacks UBIGEOs, stops at 2023 W19, and shares the same upstream CDC Perú database.

### Other published Peru datasets

#### Carcamo et al. 2025 (`healthinnovation/loreto_synchrony`)

Published alongside Carcamo, Quispe, and Carrasco-Escobar (2025), "Spatiotemporal co-distribution and time lagged cross correlation of malaria and dengue in Loreto, Peru", *PLOS Global Public Health* ([`10.1371/journal.pgph.0005598`](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0005598), CC BY 4.0). Repo [`healthinnovation/loreto_synchrony`](https://github.com/healthinnovation/loreto_synchrony) (no `LICENSE` file) ships `data/dengue.csv` (18 MB, semicolon-delimited) and `data/district_pop_1990-2022.rds`.

- **Geography, time resolution, years**: **130,598 line-listed cases** across **52 districts (6-digit UBIGEO)** in all 8 provinces of **Loreto only** (outside the pilot), spanning **2000 to 2021** with exact date (`date_not`), `year`, `month`, `week`, ICD-10 code (`cie_10`), clinical form (`diagno`), `ubigeo`, `village`, `age`, `sex`, and health facility (`clinic_name`, `clinic_cat`).
- **Provenance and caveat**: Obtained directly from **GERESA Loreto** via a formal Peru public information access request on 2023-12-01. The paper describes these as laboratory-confirmed cases dated by symptom onset, although the CSV column is named `date_not` and [`01-synchrony_plots.Rmd`](https://github.com/healthinnovation/loreto_synchrony/blob/main/01-synchrony_plots.Rmd) derives epi-weeks from `date_not`; whether `date_not` stores onset date or notification date is **UNCONFIRMED**.
- **Use for us**: **Validate (Loreto only).** This is the only genuinely independent district-by-epi-week Peru dataset we found that was not downloaded from `datosabiertos.gob.pe`. It covers only Loreto through 2021, so it cannot fill national or pilot gaps, but it can cross-check our national case file against a regional GERESA export for Loreto.

#### NOAA / US CDC Dengue Forecasting Project (Iquitos) and DrivenData DengAI

Published by the US Federal Interagency Dengue Forecasting Project ([`cdcepi/dengue-forecasting-project-2015`](https://github.com/cdcepi/dengue-forecasting-project-2015), archived on [Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.3519270`](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3519270), described in [Johansson et al. 2019, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1909865116)) and mirrored in DrivenData's [DengAI competition](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/44/dengai-predicting-disease-spread/).

- **Geography, time resolution, years**: **City of Iquitos, Peru** only (outside the pilot), weekly across 13 dengue transmission seasons (**2000/2001 to 2012/2013**, `2000-07-01` to `2013-06-25`), using custom season weeks rather than MINSA epi-weeks. Includes serotype-specific laboratory-confirmed counts (`denv1_cases` to `denv4_cases`, `other_positive_cases`, `additional_cases`, `total_cases`).
- **Provenance and license**: Active clinic-based acute febrile illness surveillance operated by **US NAMRU-6 and UC Davis** (IgM ELISA and RT-PCR; [`iquitos_metadata.md`](https://github.com/cdcepi/dengue-forecasting-project-2015/blob/master/Dengue_data/iquitos_metadata.md)). Licensed **CC BY 4.0** in `iquitos_metadata.md`.
- **Use for us**: **None.** Single non-pilot city, ends in June 2013, and measures active research-cohort enrollment rather than MINSA passive surveillance.

#### `denguedatasets` (`dengue_peru`)

The R and Python packages [`fdzul/denguedatasets`](https://github.com/fdzul/denguedatasets) and [`fdzul/denguedatasets-py`](https://github.com/fdzul/denguedatasets-py) ([Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.21499199`](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21499199), GPL-3.0) bundle `dengue_peru.csv` (12 MB, 171,281 rows).

- **Geography, time resolution, years, provenance**: National line-list with `departamento, provincia, distrito, enfermedad, ano, semana, edad, sexo` covering **2019 to 2022** only, with **no UBIGEO** and **no case class**. [`datasets.md`](https://github.com/fdzul/denguedatasets-py/blob/main/datasets.md) traces it to Kaggle ([`fazzzzzzzzzz/datos-dengue-en-el-per-2019-2022-en-csv`](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/fazzzzzzzzzz/datos-dengue-en-el-per-2019-2022-en-csv), tagged Apache 2.0), which is a stripped subset of MINSA's `datosabiertos.gob.pe` file.
- **Use for us**: **None.** Strictly inferior copy of our primary source.

#### Project Tycho v2.0 (Peru)

Three Peru dengue records from University of Pittsburgh's Project Tycho v2.0 are archived on Zenodo under **CC BY 4.0**: [Dengue 1988 to 2012 (`PE.38362002`)](https://doi.org/10.25337/T7/ptycho.v2.0/PE.38362002) (60 rows), [Dengue hemorrhagic fever 1997 to 2012 (`PE.20927009`)](https://doi.org/10.25337/T7/ptycho.v2.0/PE.20927009) (23 rows), and [Dengue without warning signs 1988 to 2005 (`PE.722862003`)](https://doi.org/10.25337/T7/ptycho.v2.0/PE.722862003) (18 rows). Direct inspection of all three CSVs confirms they are **Admin0 (national) annual counts only**, sourced from WHO DengueNet and the PAHO website.
- **Use for us**: **None.** National annual only, stops in 2012, superseded by OpenDengue.

## Open questions

1. **District-level reconciliation with Mills et al.**: Our province-by-epi-week check matched Mills's `2010_2021_cases_full_data.csv` to OpenDengue's `datosabiertos.gob.pe` extract with zero difference in 2016 and 2021, but we have not yet compared Mills's 110 pilot district triples directly against the raw `datosabiertos.gob.pe` file (which carries 6-digit UBIGEO codes). That district-level check (deferred to EDA tickets #10 and #11) will confirm whether `Distrito` in Mills's CSV equals `place of infection` in `datosabiertos.gob.pe` and quantify 2022 to 2023 revisions between Mills's May 2023 (W19) snapshot and the latest MINSA snapshot.
2. **PAHO ARBO Portal Peru Tableau workbook (`1024en/Cases`)**: Because `phip.paho.org` blocked non-browser requests (`403 Forbidden`), it remains **UNCONFIRMED** whether PAHO's live Peru country profile exposes department-level (Admin1) weekly series or only national indicators. Given that it is Tableau-only, non-commercial (`CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO`), and at best Admin1, verifying it is low priority unless national CDC Perú dashboards prove unavailable.
3. **Upstream licensing of derivative repos**: Both `cathalmills/peru_dengue_province` and `healthinnovation/loreto_synchrony` lack a GitHub `LICENSE` file even though their paper or Zenodo archive is CC BY 4.0, and OpenDengue's 2023 JSON-LD files conflict with its CC BY 4.0 Figshare/website license. Because none of these datasets will feed our production panel, this poses no blocker, but any validation script consuming them should cite the CC BY 4.0 Zenodo or Figshare DOIs directly.
4. **Loreto `date_not` semantics**: If we later cross-validate our national panel in Loreto against `healthinnovation/loreto_synchrony` (`data/dengue.csv`), we should test whether `date_not` aligns with onset week or notification week in `datosabiertos.gob.pe`.
