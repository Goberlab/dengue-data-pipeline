# Climate and ENSO sources for history and live epi-weeks in Peru

Investigation of climate (temperature, relative humidity, precipitation) and El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) data sources capable of supplying matching historical and live epi-week series for a national Peru district (6-digit UBIGEO) by epi-week panel, validated first on the pilot (Piura, Tumbes, Lambayeque). All API endpoints, catalogs, licenses, file timestamps, and CDC Perú exports were verified directly on 2026-11-23 UTC.

## Short answer

Matching historical and live paths exist for all four variable groups, though every candidate involves a trade-off between publication delay, revision frequency, spatial resolution, and account requirements.

1. **Temperature and relative humidity**: **ERA5-Land** from the Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS) is the strongest single-definition source for both history and live epi-weeks (`1950-01-01` to 5 to 6 days behind real time via `ERA5-Land-T`, `0.1°` / ~9 km, CC BY 4.0 / Copernicus Licence, free CDS account required). Relative humidity is not stored directly and must be derived hourly from 2 m temperature and 2 m dewpoint temperature before aggregating to epi-weeks. **ARCO-ERA5** on Google Cloud Public Datasets is an open, no-account fallback (`0.25°`, `1940` to 5 to 6 days behind real time).
2. **Precipitation**: No single dataset gives zero-revision, gauge-calibrated, high-resolution daily rainfall at sub-week latency. Two matched options stand out: **CHIRPS v3.0 Daily (`prelim/sat` live, `final/sat` history)** (`0.05°`, `1998` to 2 to 7 days behind real time, public domain / CC0, no account) gives gauge-blended rainfall with matching daily disaggregation (IMERG Late V07) across live and historical files, provided the panel versions preliminary and final releases by `available_at`; **ERA5-Land `total_precipitation`** (already pulled for temperature and relative humidity) offers near-zero revisions at `0.1°` but has larger orographic wet biases in the Andes. **CHIRPS v2.0** retires after December 2026, **NASA GPM IMERG V07 Final** halted at `2025-09-30` pending the V08 transition, **IMERG V07 Late** shifted into hybrid `V07C` mode on `2026-03-03`, and **SENAMHI PISCOp v3.0** (`1981-2025`, published `2026-11-14`) is a static archive with no live feed.
3. **ENSO index**: **NOAA CPC weekly OISST v2.1** (`wksst9120.for`) and **weekly relative OISST v2.1** (`rel_wksst9120.txt`) cover both **Niño 1+2** (coastal Peru) and **Niño 3.4** (central Pacific) from September 1981 to ~5 days behind real time, updated every Monday, with no account and in the US public domain. They avoid the 1-month look-ahead leakage baked into centered 3-month running means (**ONI**, **RONI**, **ICEN**, **ICENr**).
4. **CDC Perú Observatorio de Clima y Salud**: Use as an independent **cross-check only**, not as a primary climate source. Its district by epi-week export (`obs_piura_dep_total.xlsx`) starts only in `2018-SE01` (missing `2000-2017`), contains a national 4-epi-week relative humidity outage (`2025-SE19` to `2025-SE22`), truncates the live cut epi-week to `n_dias = 4` (biasing weekly rainfall sums downward), and contradicts itself on whether precipitation comes from ERA5-Land or retired IMERG V06.
5. **Leakage discipline**: Centered running means (**ONI**, **RONI**, **ICEN**, **ICENr**) average months `m-1`, `m`, and `m+1` and are not knowable until early in month `m+2`. ENFEN's operational `ICENtmp` additionally blends monthly SST forecasts. Neither should enter observed panel columns at nominal month `m`.

## Recommendations

Every variable has at least one viable source whose historical and live paths share the same definition, grid, and ingestion pipeline:

1. **Temperature (`tmean`, `tmax`, `tmin`)**: Use **ERA5-Land (Copernicus CDS `reanalysis-era5-land` or `derived-era5-land-daily-statistics`)** as the primary source (`1950` to D-5/6 via `ERA5-Land-T`, `0.1°`, CC BY 4.0 / Copernicus Licence, free CDS account). This matches CDC Perú's Observatorio choice and keeps a single physical model across 2000 to 2026+. If an environment must run with zero accounts or keys, fall back to **ARCO-ERA5 on Google Cloud Public Datasets** (`0.25°`, `1940` to D-5/6). Use **SENAMHI PISCOt v1.2** (`0.01°`, `1981-2020`) offline during EDA to quantify Andean elevation biases, and **CDC Observatorio** (`2018-SE01+`) to sanity-check district aggregation. Do not use Open-Meteo's free tier in production (non-commercial restriction and 10,000 calls/day cap) or `best_match` (2017 model splice).
2. **Relative humidity (`humr`)**: Use **ERA5-Land (Copernicus CDS `reanalysis-era5-land`)** as the primary source, computing hourly relative humidity over water from `2m_temperature` and `2m_dewpoint_temperature` before aggregating to epi-weeks (computing RH from daily mean T and Td is nonlinear and less accurate). **ARCO-ERA5 on GCS** is the no-account fallback (`0.25°`). No Peruvian gridded product publishes live relative humidity.
3. **Precipitation (`ptot`)**: Ingest **CHIRPS v3.0 Daily (`prelim/sat` live, `final/sat` history)** as the primary gauge-blended precipitation source (`0.05°`, `1998-present`, CC0, no account, polite request throttling required), storing both preliminary (`available_at` = pentad end + 2 days) and final (`available_at` = month end + ~18 days) versions in the panel so backtests replay what was known on each issue week. Keep **ERA5-Land `total_precipitation`** (already downloaded alongside temperature and dewpoint, near-zero revision) as a companion column during pilot EDA, and benchmark both against **SENAMHI PISCOp v3.0** (`1981-2025`) over Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque before deciding whether the production panel keeps one or both. Do not adopt **CHIRPS v2.0** (retiring December 2026) or **IMERG V07 Final** (ended `2025-09-30`); re-evaluate **NASA GPM IMERG V08** once retrospective V08 Final and Late releases are published.
4. **ENSO index (coastal and central Pacific)**: Use **NOAA CPC Weekly OISST v2.1 (`wksst9120.for`)** and **Weekly Relative OISST v2.1 (`rel_wksst9120.txt`)** as the primary panel ENSO series. They deliver both **Niño 1+2** (critical for Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque coastal El Niño dynamics) and **Niño 3.4** (teleconnections to the Andes and Amazon) on a single non-centered weekly cadence from September 1981 to ~5 days behind real time, with no account and public-domain licensing. Include the relative series (`rel_wksst9120.txt`) because secular tropical ocean warming has created a `+0.44 °C` mean divergence between legacy ONI and RONI since 2020 (verified directly from CPC ASCII files). Optionally add unsmoothed monthly **ERSSTv6** series (**IGP `ecindex_ersstv6.txt`** and **CPC `Rnino34.ascii.txt`** / **PSL `nina1.anom.data`**) stamped at their actual monthly publication dates. Never ingest centered **ONI**, **RONI**, **ICEN**, or **ICENr** stamped at nominal month `m`, and never ingest `ICENtmp`.

## Audit of CDC Perú Observatorio de Clima y Salud

We inspected the downloaded department export `obs_piura_dep_total.xlsx` (3 sheets: `Departamento` with 470 rows, `Provincia` with 3,760 rows, and `Distrito` with 30,550 rows covering all 65 Piura districts), the single-district export `obs_piura_dist_total.xlsx` (470 rows for Piura `200101`), the national export `obs_nacional_total.xlsx` (1,391 rows), and the Shiny app HTML (`https://app7.dge.gob.pe/maps2/shiny_observatorio_web/`).

### Schema, units, and coverage

Each district row carries `Departamento`, `Provincia`, `Distrito`, `UBIGEO`, `Ano`, `Semana`, `Tipo caso`, `Casos`, and eleven climate columns:

- Observed weekly climate: `tmean` (°C, Piura range `10.5` to `29.4`), `tmax` (°C, `14.1` to `36.3`), `tmin` (°C, `5.5` to `25.2`), `humr` (%, `42.2` to `95.4`), `ptot` (mm, `0.0` to `232.2`), and `n_dias` (number of days included in the epi-week).
- Weekly climatological normals: `tmean_clima`, `tmax_clima`, `tmin_clima`, `humr_clima`, `ptot_clima`.

Observed climate runs from **`2018-SE01` through `2026-SE45`** (`Fecha de corte: 14-11-2026`, `Actualización: 18-11-2026`). Future epi-weeks (`2026-SE46` to `SE52`) are present as rows with null climate values, non-null climatologies, and `Casos = 0`. There are no rows before `2018-SE01`, so the export cannot cover the 2000 to 2017 historical dengue archive.

### Data defects and internal contradictions

Direct inspection revealed four defects that disqualify Observatorio as a primary climate source:

1. **Live partial-epi-week truncation (`n_dias = 4`)**: In `2026-SE45` (Sunday `2026-11-08` to Saturday `2026-11-14`), every single one of the 65 Piura districts (and the national rollup) has `n_dias = 4`. Because the export was updated on `2026-11-18` while upstream ERA5-Land lags by ~5 to 6 days, CDC aggregated only the first four days (`2026-11-08` to `2026-11-11`). Summing `ptot` over 4 days instead of 7 systematically understates rainfall in the live cut epi-week until silently overwritten the following epi-week.
2. **National 4-epi-week relative humidity gap**: `humr` is null for all 65 Piura districts (260 rows) and in `obs_nacional_total.xlsx` across four consecutive epi-weeks: **`2025-SE19`, `2025-SE20`, `2025-SE21`, and `2025-SE22`**, while `tmean`, `tmax`, `tmin`, and `ptot` remain populated. No explanation appears in the app UI.
3. **Contradictory source attribution inside the app**: The `Información` tab attributes precipitation to `NASA GPM IMERG` and links to the retired V06 endpoint (`https://disc.gsfc.nasa.gov/datasets/GPM_3IMERGDF_06/summary`), whereas both the `Tendencias` tab footers and the `Módulo de Clima` footers cite `ERA5-Land hourly data. Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)` for all variables including precipitation. Likewise, `Tendencias` defines normals over `1981-2020`, whereas `Módulo de Clima` defines them over `2001-2020`. Because `ptot` shares `n_dias = 4` in `2026-SE45` (when IMERG Late already covered all 7 days through Nov 14), the exported `ptot` is consistent with ERA5-Land or a single ERA5-Land date mask rather than live IMERG.
4. **Empirical verification against Open-Meteo ERA5-Land**: Comparing Piura district (`200101`) in `obs_piura_dist_total.xlsx` against Open-Meteo `era5_land` and `era5` over `2026-SE40` to `SE45` confirms that Observatorio's temperature and humidity track ERA5-Land closely (`SE40`: Observatorio `tmean = 27.2 °C`, `tmax = 34.0 °C`, `tmin = 23.1 °C` vs Open-Meteo ERA5-Land `tmean = 27.27 °C`, weekly mean of daily max `34.09 °C`, weekly mean of daily min `23.16 °C`; `SE45` over Nov 8 to 11 only: Observatorio `28.2 / 34.4 / 24.3 °C` vs ERA5-Land `28.40 / 34.53 / 24.48 °C`), whereas `0.25°` ERA5 diverges (`tmax = 32.37 °C`, `ptot = 5.2` to `14.0 mm` vs Observatorio `0.0` to `0.9 mm`). This also proves Observatorio's `tmax` and `tmin` are weekly means of daily extremes, not weekly absolute extremes.

**Verdict**: Retain Observatorio climate columns solely as a **secondary cross-check** when validating our own district-level ERA5-Land aggregation for 2018 onward. Fetch climate directly from upstream primary archives so the pipeline controls the full 2000 to 2026+ history, avoids silent partial-epi-week truncation, avoids the `2025-SE19..22` humidity outage, and stamps exact `available_at` values.

## Candidate profiles

### ERA5, ERA5T, ERA5-Land, and ERA5-Land-T (Copernicus CDS and cloud mirrors)

- **Variables**: Hourly 2 m air temperature (`2m_temperature`), 2 m dewpoint temperature (`2m_dewpoint_temperature`), and `total_precipitation` (plus soil moisture, wind, radiation). Relative humidity is not archived directly on single levels; ECMWF documents computing near-surface RH from 2 m temperature and 2 m dewpoint temperature with respect to saturation over water ([ERA5 CKB](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation)).
- **Resolution and coverage**: **ERA5** runs globally at `0.25°` (~28 to 31 km) hourly from `1940-01-01` to present ([Hersbach et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3803); [CDS](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-single-levels)). **ERA5-Land** replays the land surface model offline at `0.1°` (~9 km, oceans masked) hourly from `1950-01-01` to present, forced by ERA5 atmospheric fields with elevation lapse-rate correction ([Muñoz-Sabater et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-13-4349-2021); [CDS](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-land)). CDS also serves pre-aggregated daily statistics (`derived-era5-land-daily-statistics`, `derived-era5-single-levels-daily-statistics`) ([CDS daily](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/derived-era5-land-daily-statistics)).
- **Publication delay**: Verified live on `2026-11-23` via CDS STAC catalogue JSON: all four collections (`reanalysis-era5-single-levels`, `reanalysis-era5-land`, and both daily statistics collections) were updated `2026-11-23T00:00:00Z` with temporal extent through **`2026-11-17T00:00:00Z`** (6-day lag). ECMWF documents nominal daily updates ~5 days behind real time (typically by 12 UTC at D-5) for both `ERA5T` and `ERA5-Land-T` ([ERA5-Land CKB](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5-Land%3A+data+documentation)).
- **First release vs final value**: Initial near-real-time releases (`ERA5T` and `ERA5-Land-T`, GRIB `expver=0005`) are overwritten in place on CDS by consolidated `ERA5` / `ERA5-Land` (`expver=0001`) roughly 2 to 3 months after the target month. Under normal operations, **first release and final values are bit-for-bit or meteorologically identical**, because both run the exact same model and assimilation system. Revisions occur only when serious assimilation bugs are discovered; documented global rewrite incidents on ECMWF CKB are `2021-09-01` to `2021-12-13` (central Asia snow assimilation), `2022-10` (Cray to ATOS migration, meteorologically negligible), `2024-07-01` to `2024-11-17` (Alps snow assimilation), and **`2025-01-01 12 UTC` to `2025-01-06 12 UTC`**, when missing satellite observations caused small systematic errors mainly in **tropical humidity** and both `ERA5T` and `ERA5-Land-T` were rewritten ([ERA5 CKB](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation); [ERA5-Land CKB](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5-Land%3A+data+documentation)). Note also two implementation traps: (a) ERA5-Land hourly precipitation is accumulated from `00 UTC` to forecast step `01..24` (requiring differencing between consecutive hours, unlike ERA5 single-hour accumulations); (b) CDS monthly averaged ERA5-Land accumulated fields from September 2022 to February 2024 contain an unreplaced bug (always aggregate from hourly or daily statistics, never the CDS monthly averaged accumulated dataset).
- **Access and license**: Direct CDS retrieval requires a free Copernicus CDS account and API token (`POST` to `/api/retrieve/v1/processes/reanalysis-era5-land/execution` verified `401 authentication required`). Two important mirrors exist: **Google Cloud Public Datasets ARCO-ERA5** (`gs://gcp-public-data-arco-era5/ar/full_37-1h-0p25deg-chunk-1.zarr-v3`, [GitHub](https://github.com/google-research/arco-era5)) serves `0.25°` ERA5 (`2m_temperature`, `2m_dewpoint_temperature`, `total_precipitation`) with **no account or key**, verified updated `2026-11-23 08:13Z` with `valid_time_stop: 2026-06-30` and `valid_time_stop_era5t: 2026-11-17`; **Google Earth Engine** serves `0.1°` ERA5-Land (`ECMWF/ERA5_LAND/HOURLY` verified live to `2026-11-17T22:00Z`, `DAILY_AGGR` to `2026-11-15`), requiring a GEE account (free for non-commercial use only). Underlying ERA5 / ERA5-Land data are licensed under the **Licence to Use Copernicus Products** (free, worldwide, perpetual, commercial use allowed with attribution) and tagged `CC-BY-4.0` in CDS STAC metadata ([CDS Licence](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/licences/licence-to-use-copernicus-products)).

### Open-Meteo Archive API

- **Variables and empirical gap**: Serves hourly and daily `temperature_2m`, `relative_humidity_2m`, `dew_point_2m`, and `precipitation` at point coordinates ([Historical Weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)). Live queries on `2026-11-23` across four Peruvian locations (Piura city, Morropón, Iquitos, Cusco) confirmed a critical limitation: **`models=era5_land` returns `null` for `precipitation_sum` and hourly `precipitation` everywhere in Peru**. The model `era5_seamless` merges `0.1°` ERA5-Land temperature and humidity with `0.25°` ERA5 precipitation. The default `best_match` stitches 9 km ECMWF IFS (`2017` to present, 0-day delay) onto pre-2017 ERA5/ERA5-Land, creating a regime discontinuity in 2017.
- **Resolution, downscaling, and latency**: Point queries (not polygon zonal averages) that apply 90 m DEM statistical elevation downscaling by default unless `&elevation=nan` is passed. Verified live on `2026-11-23`: `era5_land` and `era5` daily series ended at `2026-11-16` (7-day lag; hourly `era5_land` reached `2026-11-17T18:00` local time), while `best_match` reached `2026-11-23`. Exact timing of when Open-Meteo re-ingests consolidated ERA5 overwrites is `UNCONFIRMED`.
- **Access and license**: Free REST API needs no API key but is strictly **non-commercial only** and rate-limited to 10,000 calls/day with no uptime SLA ([Terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms); [Pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing)). Commercial or high-volume national use requires a paid API key. Weather data are CC BY 4.0; server code is AGPLv3 ([Licence](https://open-meteo.com/en/licence)). Useful for point prototyping, not for national raster production.

### CHIRPS v3.0 and v2.0 (preliminary and final)

- **Variables, resolution, and coverage**: Precipitation only, `0.05°` (~5.5 km) over land, `60°S` to `60°N` (expanded from `50°S-50°N` in v2.0), from `1981-01-01` to near-real time at pentadal, dekadal, monthly, and daily cadences ([CHC CHIRPS v3](https://www.chc.ucsb.edu/data/chirps3); [Funk et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07096-4)). **CHIRPS v2.0 production ends after December 2026** ([CHC CHIRPS v2](https://www.chc.ucsb.edu/data/chirps)), so any new pipeline must target **CHIRPS v3.0**.
- **Daily disaggregation structure**: CHIRPS is natively pentadal and monthly; daily CHIRPS v3.0 distributes each pentad total across its constituent days using daily ratios from either **ERA5** (`rnl`, `1981-present`, resampled from `0.25°`) or **NASA IMERG Late V07** (`sat`, `1998-present`, resampled from `0.1°`) ([CHIRPS v3 archive](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/)). Crucially, **preliminary daily CHIRPS v3.0 is only produced in the `sat` (IMERG Late) flavor**. Therefore, matching live and historical daily disaggregation requires pairing `prelim/sat` with `final/sat` (`1998-present`, which covers our 2000+ dengue record). When the disaggregation source has zero pentad rain while CHIRPS is positive, rainfall is split evenly across days.
- **Publication delay and observed timestamps**: Preliminary CHIRPS v3.0 is released **2 days after each pentad end** (on the 2nd, 7th, 12th, 17th, 22nd, and 27th of each month), giving a 2-to-7-day lag. Verified directly in CHC's `2026/` listings: `chirps-v3.0.prelim.2026.10.31.tif` was posted `2026-11-02 20:31`, and the `2026-11-16..20` pentad was posted `2026-11-22 17:04`. Final CHIRPS v3.0 is released once per month in week 3 of the following month: October 2026 `final/rnl` posted `2026-11-13 21:31` (13 days after month end) and `final/sat` posted `2026-11-18 16:44` (18 days after month end).
- **Preliminary vs final revisions and gaps**: Preliminary CHIRPS v3.0 blends rapid GTS, GHCN, SSOD, and select regional feeds (SISSA, IDEAM, DMC, etc., with lighter QC), whereas Final CHIRPS v3.0 incorporates 90+ quality-controlled station sources (~4x v2.0) plus Legates-Willmott gauge-undercatch corrections (making v3.0 systematically wetter than v2.0). CHC publishes monthly South America difference maps under `diagnostics/chirps_minus_prelim/`, though exact Peru-only prelim-vs-final RMSE/MAE could not be computed here due to CHC HTTP `429` throttling (`UNCONFIRMED` exact Peru magnitude; to measure in EDA). One important post-2022 caveat in CHC's `diagnostics/readme.txt`: missing or erroneous geostationary IR data were gap-filled with unbiased ERA5 for `1981-2022`, but **from 2023 onward missing IR pixels are left as `-9999`** (the pipeline must check for `-9999` fill values).
- **Access and license**: Direct HTTPS GeoTIFF and NetCDF files from `https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/`, **no account or API key required**. CHC's nginx server enforces aggressive rate limiting (bursty directory probes returned `429 Too Many Requests`), so downloads must be polite and serialized. Licensed **public domain / CC0** ([Funk et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07096-4)).

### NASA GPM IMERG V07 (Early, Late, Final) and V08 transition

- **Variables, resolution, and coverage**: Precipitation rate and accumulation (`0.1°` / ~11 km, global `90°S-90°N` in V07, half-hourly, daily, and monthly), continuous TRMM+GPM record starting `1998-01-01` ([NASA GPM IMERG](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/imerg)).
- **Publication delay and live status (critical 2026 finding)**: Verified live via NASA CMR granule queries on `2026-11-23`:
  - **Early (`GPM_3IMERGDE`)**: ~4.5-hour latency (`2026-11-22` daily granule updated `2026-11-23T04:28:18Z`), forward passive-microwave morphing only, seasonal climatological gauge calibration.
  - **Late (`GPM_3IMERGDL`)**: ~14.5-hour latency (`2026-11-22` daily granule updated `2026-11-23T14:28:28Z`), forward + backward morphing, seasonal climatological gauge calibration to Final.
  - **Final (`GPM_3IMERGDF`, `GPM_3IMERGHH`, `GPM_3IMERGM`)**: Normally ~3.5 to 4 months behind real time (waits for GPCC monthly gauge analysis and ERA5), **but V07 Final explicitly ended on `2025-09-30`** (last updated `2026-02-02`).
- **First release vs final and V08 transition discontinuity**: Per NASA GPM's official [IMERG V08 Transition Schedule](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/imerg-v08-transition-schedule) (published `2026-04-28`), NASA stopped V07 Final at September 2025 because upstream parent retrievals (CORRA and GPROF) upgraded to V08 and mixing V08 inputs into V07 Final would create discontinuities. Meanwhile, **Early and Late transitioned on `2026-03-03` from `V07B` to `V07C` ("hybrid" mode)**, verified in CMR granule metadata, running the V07 algorithm on GPROF V08 inputs with seasonal climatological CORRA calibration. NASA explicitly warns of potential discontinuities during hybrid mode until retrospective V08 Final, Late, and Early records (1998 to present) are released (targeted no sooner than fall 2026; not yet live on CMR on `2026-11-23`). Separately, literature comparing Early, Late, and Final runs (e.g., [O et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-21-6559-2017)) shows Late reduces random error relative to Early via backward morphing, while Final removes substantial regional bias via monthly GPCC gauge adjustment; raw Early and Late tend to exhibit larger daily RMSE and systematic wet/dry biases in steep Andean terrain.
- **Access and license**: **Free account required** on both official distribution channels: GES DISC HTTPS/OPeNDAP redirects (`302`) to NASA Earthdata Login (`urs.earthdata.nasa.gov`), and PPS GIS GeoTIFF directories (`https://jsimpsonhttps.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/imerg/gis/late/`) return `401 Unauthorized` (`Basic realm="PPS NRT Private"`). Data are NASA open data (not copyrighted in the US; free reuse including commercial with attribution and no NASA endorsement) ([NASA ESDIS Data Use Guidance](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software-policies/data-use-guidance)).

### SENAMHI PISCO (PISCOp v3.0, PISCOp v2.1, PISCOt v1.2)

- **Variables, resolution, and coverage**:
  - **PISCOp v3.0** (precipitation, published `2026-11-14` by L. Gutierrez and W. Lavado-Casimiro): daily and monthly NetCDF grids, `0.1°` (~10 km), `1981-2025`, blending quality-controlled SENAMHI and neighboring-country rain gauges with satellite/radar-gauge precipitation predictors ([Figshare DOI `10.6084/m9.figshare.32411886.v2`](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/High-resolution_grids_of_rainfall_for_Peru_-_PISCOp_v3_0_dataset/32411886)). Supersedes **PISCOp v2.1** (`1981-2016` stable, [Aybar et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/02626667.2019.1649411)).
  - **PISCOt v1.2** (daily maximum and minimum air temperature, published `2023-05-30`): `0.01°` (~1 km) daily and monthly normal NetCDF grids, `1981-2020`, blending homogenized SENAMHI stations with MODIS land surface temperature and elevation ([Huerta et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02777-w); [Figshare DOI `10.6084/m9.figshare.c.5959863.v3`](https://figshare.com/collections/High-resolution_grids_of_daily_air_temperature_for_Peru_-_the_PISCOt_v1_2_dataset/5959863)).
  - **No gridded relative humidity product** exists in the PISCO family.
- **Live path, latency, and revisions**: **No live weekly operational feed exists** for either PISCOp or PISCOt. Legacy IRI Data Library endpoints (`https://iridl.ldeo.columbia.edu/SOURCES/.SENAMHI/.HSR/.PISCO/`), which previously mirrored PISCOp v2.1 `stable` and `unstable` directories, now redirect (`302`) to `dlauth` login and cannot be checked without an account; even historically, IRI's `unstable` operational updates suffered multi-week/multi-month pauses. Both PISCOp v3.0 and PISCOt v1.2 are static multi-decade releases on Figshare.
- **Access and license**: Direct HTTPS download from Figshare with **no account or key**. Note a major license difference verified via Figshare's API: **PISCOt v1.2 is `CC BY 4.0`**, whereas **PISCOp v3.0 is licensed `GPL 3.0+`** (`https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html`). Treat PISCO as an offline validation benchmark for Peru biases rather than a production pipeline input.

### SENAMHI meteorological station network

- **Variables, coverage, and live status**: SENAMHI operates national conventional and automatic meteorological and hydrological stations reporting daily/hourly temperature, precipitation, and (at a subset of stations) relative humidity via `https://www.senamhi.gob.pe/site/descarga-datos/`, `https://observatorio.senamhi.gob.pe/investigadores/red-estaciones`, and `https://idesep.senamhi.gob.pe/portalidesep/`. Exact counts of active stations with continuous 2000 to 2026 coverage per district and exact live latency are `UNCONFIRMED`, as station density drops sharply in the high Andes (>3,500 m) and Amazon lowlands ([Aybar et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/02626667.2019.1649411); [Huerta et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02777-w)).
- **Revisions, access, and license**: Real-time station tables are unvalidated telemetry subject to later offline QC; historical bulk downloads require user registration, CAPTCHA verification, and per-query time caps (no public unauthenticated bulk station-series REST API was found; `https://servicios.senamhi.gob.pe/api/geo` returned `404` on root, and `datosabiertos.gob.pe` blocked automated probes with CloudWAF `418`). Explicit open license terms for raw station exports are `UNCONFIRMED`. Unsuitable as a national district-level pipeline source because most of Peru's 1,893 districts lack a continuous station and automated live ingestion is gated.

### NOAA CPC and PSL ENSO indices (Niño 1+2, Niño 3.4, ONI, RONI)

- **Variables and coverage**:
  - **Weekly OISST v2.1** (`0.25°` satellite + in-situ SST): `wksst9120.for` (`1981-09-02` to present) and relative anomalies `rel_wksst9120.txt` (`1981-09-09` to present) give both **Niño 1+2** (`0-10°S, 90°W-80°W`) and **Niño 3.4** (`5°N-5°S, 170°W-120°W`) on a 1991-2020 base ([CPC indices directory](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/data/indices/)).
  - **Monthly OISST v2.1**: `sstoi.indices` (`1982-01` to present) and `rel_mthsst9120.txt` (`1981-09` to present) cover all four Niño boxes.
  - **Monthly ERSSTv6** (`2° x 2°` in-situ ANN reconstruction, [Huang et al. 2025a](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-23-0707.1), [2025b](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-24-0185.1)): Effective **`2026-02-01`**, NOAA CPC replaced ONI with **RONI (Relative Oceanic Niño Index)** as its official ENSO metric ([NWS PNS 26-05](https://www.weather.gov/media/notification/pdf_2026/pns26-05_Relative_ONI.pdf)), subtracting global tropical mean SST anomaly (`20°N-20°S`) from Niño 3.4 and rescaling to match traditional variance ([CPC RONI](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso/roni/)). CPC also upgraded ONI and RONI to **ERSSTv6** (`oni.ascii.txt`, `RONI.ascii.txt`, and unsmoothed monthly `Rnino34.ascii.txt` from `1950` to `2026-10`), while legacy `ersst5.nino.mth.91-20.ascii` stopped updating after **`2026-06`** (`Last-Modified: 2026-08-05`). For monthly ERSSTv6 **Niño 1+2**, NOAA PSL serves `https://psl.noaa.gov/data/correlation/nina1.anom.data` (`1948-2026`, 1981-2010 base).
- **Publication delay and revisions**: Weekly files update every Monday (~5-day lag; verified `2026-11-23` through `18NOV2026`). Underlying NCEI OISST v2.1 files are preliminary at ~1-day lag and finalized at **14 days** once delayed buoys, ships, and Argo floats arrive ([NCEI OISST](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/optimum-interpolation-sst); [Huang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-20-0166.1)), so the newest 1 to 2 weekly rows can revise slightly. Monthly ERSSTv6 files update by the 3rd to 5th of the next month (`2026-11-03` for October 2026 / `ASO 2026`), and CPC explicitly warns that because of ERSSTv6's high-frequency filter, **values may change for up to 2 months after initial real-time posting** ([CPC RONI](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso/roni/)).
- **Quantified cross-product differences (verified locally on CPC files)**:
  - Monthly OISST v2.1 (`sstoi.indices`) vs ERSSTv5 (`1982-01` to `2026-06`, `n = 534` months): Niño 1+2 MAE = **`0.217 °C`** (max `0.95 °C`), Niño 3.4 MAE = **`0.121 °C`** (max `0.47 °C`). Thus OISST and ERSST are **not interchangeable** without a definition break, especially in Niño 1+2.
  - Legacy ONI minus RONI (`1950` to `2026`, `n = 921` seasons): full-record mean `-0.002 °C`, but **`2020-2026` mean `+0.442 °C`** (max abs `0.76 °C`; `ASO 2026`: ONI `+2.46 °C` vs RONI `+1.97 °C`), quantifying the tropical ocean warming drift that motivated NOAA's switch to relative indices.
- **Access and license**: Direct HTTPS plain-text ASCII files, **no account or key**, US federal public domain.

### ENFEN and IGP coastal indices (ICEN, ICENr, ICENtmp, E and C)

- **Variables and coverage**: Maintained by Instituto Geofísico del Perú (IGP) for ENFEN at `http://met.igp.gob.pe/variabclim/indices.html` (updated `2026-09-09`):
  - **Official ICEN (`http://met.igp.gob.pe/datos/ICEN.txt`)**: 3-month centered running mean of Niño 1+2 SST anomaly per Nota Técnica ENFEN 01-2024 (December 2024), monthly `1950-01` to `2026-09` (`Last-Modified: 2026-11-04`). Verified against CPC series over `2000-2026`: matches 3-month centered ERSSTv5 1991-2020 with MAE `0.021 °C` through May 2026 (`0.00 °C` diff in Apr/May 2026) and diverges from OISST v2.1 by `0.07` to `0.17 °C` in Jun to Sep 2026 (`ICEN.txt` reaches `+4.50 °C` in Sep 2026). Note: the Nota Técnica ENFEN 01-2024 PDF is hosted on `enfen.imarpe.gob.pe`, which is behind a Cloudflare challenge and could not be read directly (`UNCONFIRMED` exact post-June-2026 SST input switch inside official `ICEN.txt` after CPC's `ersst5` file stalled).
  - **Traditional ICEN (`ICEN_81_10.txt`, `ICEN_91_20.txt`)**: Unofficial ENFEN 2012 definitions on ERSSTv5 (`Last-Modified: 2026-10-19`, trailing at `2026-08`).
  - **Relative Coastal Index `ICENr` (`http://met.igp.gob.pe/datos/ICENr.txt`)**: Unofficial relative Niño 1+2 centered 3-month mean removing non-ENSO tropical warming ([Reupo et al. 2024](https://repositorio.igp.gob.pe/items/5d24a0bd-21d6-4365-9f45-cbe4c08f1ae2)), `1950-01` to `2026-09` (`Last-Modified: 2026-11-17`).
  - **Unsmoothed E and C indices (`http://met.igp.gob.pe/datos/ecindex_ersstv6.txt`)**: Monthly Eastern Pacific (`E`) and Central Pacific (`C`) orthogonal EOF indices on ERSSTv6 with **no 3-month smoothing** ([Takahashi et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011GL047364)), updated `2026-11-23 15:47Z` through `2026-10`.

Both ICEN and `ecindex_ersstv6.txt` also revise slightly as NOAA updates upstream ERSST.
- **Access and license**: Direct HTTP plain-text files on `met.igp.gob.pe` with **no account or key** (filenames are case-sensitive: uppercase `ICEN.txt` returns `200`, lowercase `icen.txt` returns `404`; `enfen.imarpe.gob.pe` is behind Cloudflare and `imarpe.gob.pe` returned `403`). No explicit open-data license is attached to the `.txt` files (`UNCONFIRMED`).

## Leakage flags

1. **Centered 3-month running means (`ONI`, `RONI`, `ICEN`, `ICENr`) leak 1 month of future ocean state**: The value labeled `2026-09` (`ASO`) is the mean of August, September, and October SST anomalies. It does not exist until October ends and NOAA/IGP publish early in November (`2026-11-03` for CPC ONI/RONI; `2026-11-04` for IGP ICEN). Stamping `2026-09` with `available_at = 2026-10-01` leaks all of October into issue weeks during October. **Non-centered alternatives**: ingest **NOAA CPC Weekly OISST v2.1 (`wksst9120.for` and `rel_wksst9120.txt`)** for Niño 1+2 and Niño 3.4, plus optionally unsmoothed monthly **CPC `Rnino34.ascii.txt`**, **PSL `nina1.anom.data`**, and **IGP `ecindex_ersstv6.txt`**. If a smoothed 3-month feature is desired, the downstream modeler can compute a strictly causal trailing average over the past 3 months (or 12 epi-weeks). If official ONI/RONI/ICEN are ever kept for stakeholder reporting, their `available_at` must be set to the actual post-`(m+1)` publication date (at least `m+2` day 5).
2. **`ICENtmp` mixes forecasts into observations**: Because `ICENtmp` uses ENFEN consensus monthly forecasts for unobserved months, ingesting `ICENtmp` values from ENFEN communiqués or dashboards into an observed ENSO column leaks forecast assumptions and creates an inconsistent definition between live issue weeks (forecast-blended) and historical issue weeks (final observed). Exclude `ICENtmp` from observed ENSO columns; forecasts belong under ticket #22.
3. **Partial live epi-weeks (`n_dias < 7`) distort weekly totals**: Because ERA5-Land lags by 5 to 6 days, an issue week run on Tuesday or Wednesday after a Saturday epi-week close (like CDC Observatorio's Wednesday `2026-11-18` update for `2026-SE45` ending `2026-11-14`) has only 4 of 7 days available. Averaging temperature over 4 days adds noise, and summing precipitation over 4 days creates a `-43%` structural downward bias relative to complete historical epi-weeks. Either schedule the weekly climate pull once all 7 days of the closed epi-week are published (by Friday/Saturday UTC, 6 to 7 days after Saturday close), or stamp `n_dias` explicitly and leave incomplete epi-weeks null until complete.
4. **Silent historical-to-live model splices**: Avoid **Open-Meteo `best_match`** (switches from ERA5/ERA5-Land to 9 km ECMWF IFS in 2017), **Open-Meteo `era5_seamless`** (mixes `0.1°` ERA5-Land temperature/humidity with `0.25°` ERA5 precipitation), mixing **OISST** live with **ERSST** history (MAE `0.22 °C` in Niño 1+2), and mixing **IMERG V07 Final** (`1998-2025-09`) with **IMERG V07C Late** (`2026-03+`).
5. **Preliminary-to-final revisions require versioned `available_at` snapshots**: CHIRPS v3.0 (`prelim/sat` -> `final/sat`), OISST v2.1 (14-day prelim -> final), ERSSTv6 (2-month filter revisions), and rare ERA5T/ERA5-Land-T rewrites all modify previously published periods. The panel's multi-version `available_at` contract handles these cleanly **only if** the pipeline appends revised versions with their new publication `available_at` instead of overwriting old versions in place.

## Peru-specific biases

Peru spans three sharply contrasting climate regimes, and every global gridded product has documented regional errors relevant to a national panel validated first on the pilot (Piura, Tumbes, Lambayeque).

### Arid Pacific coast and pilot (Piura, Tumbes, Lambayeque)

- **Coastal fog (*garúa*) and non-convective drizzle**: Along the cold Humboldt Current coast, shallow stratus and fog produce low-level moisture and cooling without cold cloud tops. Thermal-infrared satellite estimates (**CHIRPS**) and passive-microwave retrievals (**IMERG**) miss light coastal drizzle; even station-interpolated **PISCOt v1.2** shows 2 to 6 °C daily differences between fog-covered and cloud-free coastal days ([Huerta et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02777-w)).
- **El Niño Costero extremes**: Extreme rainfall and flooding in Piura, Tumbes, and Lambayeque are tightly coupled to **Niño 1+2** warming rather than central Pacific Niño 3.4 alone ([Lagos et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.5194/adgeo-14-231-2008); [Takahashi et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011GL047364); [Sulca et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.5185)). Coarse reanalysis (`0.25°` ERA5) smooths intense localized convective coastal storms and mixes ocean and land boundary conditions along the narrow coastal plain; even in our `2026-SE40..44` Piura check, `0.25°` ERA5 produced `5.2` to `14.0 mm/week` of rainfall when `0.1°` ERA5-Land / Observatorio recorded `0.0` to `0.9 mm`.
- **ERA5-Land ocean mask**: ERA5-Land masks ocean cells, so small coastal or island districts must handle nearest-land-cell or valid-pixel polygon averaging carefully to avoid null values.

### Steep Andes

- **Elevation and lapse-rate mismatch**: Many Peruvian districts span 2,000 to 4,000+ m of relief within a single polygon (including inland Ayabaca and Huancabamba in Piura). Even at `0.1°` (~9 km), ERA5-Land cannot resolve narrow inhabited valleys vs glaciated ridges. On four Peruvian Andean glaciers, [Bonshoms et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11629-022-7388-4) found strong ERA5-Land 2 m temperature correlation (`r > 0.80`), but much noisier relative humidity (`r = 0.4` to `0.6` hourly, `RMSE = 3%` to `13%` across wet vs dry outer-tropical sites).
- **Orographic precipitation wet bias and satellite errors**: Reanalyses (**ERA5** and **ERA5-Land**) systematically overestimate precipitation over steep tropical mountain barriers due to coarse orographic lift ([Lavers et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.4351)). Conversely, satellite products (**IMERG** and **CHIRPS**) exhibit elevated RMSE, poor daily correlation, orographic mislocation, and snow/rain misclassification over the Peruvian Andes and Altiplano ([Manz et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015JD023788); [Manz et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1175/JHM-D-16-0277.1); [Satgé et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atmosres.2015.07.012)). Even gauge-blended **PISCOp** and **PISCOt** degrade above 3,500 to 4,000 m where SENAMHI gauges are scarce ([Aybar et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/02626667.2019.1649411); [Imfeld et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.6645)).
- **Implication for district aggregation**: Plain area-weighted averaging over steep Andean districts blends uninhabited 4,000 m peaks with lower-elevation dengue-bearing valleys; population-weighted zonal statistics (using WorldPop or INEI gridded population) should be evaluated against plain district means during EDA.

### Amazon lowlands and eastern Andean slopes

- **Gauge scarcity**: SENAMHI rain-gauge and thermometer density is lowest in Loreto, Ucayali, Madre de Dios, and the eastern Andean-Amazon transition slopes, increasing interpolation uncertainty in **PISCO** and weakening station anchoring in **CHIRPS** ([Aybar et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/02626667.2019.1649411); [Huerta et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02777-w); [Funk et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07096-4)).
- **Convective precipitation and reanalysis trends**: **ERA5** exhibits documented tropical precipitation biases and dry-season artifacts over parts of the Amazon, while **IMERG** captures convective systems well at sub-daily scales but can misplace diurnal peaks and saturate heavy convective cores. **CHIRPS v3.0** post-2022 `-9999` un-filled IR gaps must also be monitored over persistent Amazon cloud cover.
